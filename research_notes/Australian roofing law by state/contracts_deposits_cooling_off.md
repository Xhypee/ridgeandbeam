## Q1. Governing Acts and regulators (all 8 jurisdictions)

### Takeaway
Each state and territory has its own home-building contract statute and regulator. VIC's regulator changed (VBA → Building and Plumbing Commission, BPC) and NSW has a Building Commission NSW inside the NSW Government (nsw.gov.au) alongside Fair Trading. NSW also passed a new 2026 Act that has mostly not yet commenced.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW:** Home Building Act 1989 (NSW). Part 6 (insurance) "remains the insurance statute" until the new *Building (Approvals and Practitioners) Act 2026* commences. The 2026 Act reportedly received Royal Assent 14 Aug 2026, with most provisions commencing on dates to be proclaimed (manufactured-homes sections commenced on assent) — [Feasly, secondary](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/home-warranty-insurance-nsw-developer-guide); [Holding Redlich, secondary](https://www.holdingredlich.com/building-approvals-and-practitioners-bill-2026-insights-part-3-of-3-duty-of-care)
- **NSW:** The Statute Law (Miscellaneous Provisions) Bill 2026 proposes to defer repeal of the Home Building Regulation 2014 because "The Building Commission NSW intends to progress legislative amendments to certain building legislation in 2026, including the Home Building Regulation 2014" — [Mondaq, secondary](https://www.mondaq.com/australia/construction-planning/1794260/more-changes-to-building-legislation-quietly-flagged)
- **NSW:** Official guidance on contracts now sits on nsw.gov.au (Building Commission NSW / Fair Trading content) — [NSW Gov: Contracts for residential building work](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/building-or-renovating-a-home/preparing/contracts); [NSW Gov: Guide to providing home building contracts](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/your-obligations-to-your-customers/guide-to-providing-home-building-contracts); [Fair Trading: Guide to home building contracts](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/construction-and-trade-essentials/guide-to-home-building-contracts)
- **NSW (unreliable claim, flag):** A blog claims a "Home Building Amendment (Fair Trading and Building Legislation) Act 2026" with a 10-year statutory warranty and new cooling-off rules. Not corroborated by any other source; treat as unreliable — [Gianta, secondary/unverified](https://www.gianta.com.au/blog/approvals-9/home-building-act-nsw-2026-what-the-fair-trading-and-building-legislation-amendment-bill-means-for-your-project-90)
- **VIC:** Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995 (DBC Act), e.g. s 11 "Limit on amount of deposit" — [AustLII DBC Act s 11](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dbca1995275/s11.html). Regulator for insurance is now the **Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC)**, which provides the new Home Warranty scheme — [BPC: Home Warranty](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/insurance-for-domestic-building-work/home-warranty); [BPC: DBI and Home Warranty](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/insurance-for-domestic-building-work/domestic-building-insurance-and-home-warranty). Consumer Affairs Victoria (CAV) still publishes deposit/contract guidance — [CAV deposits and payments (builders)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/builders-and-tradespeople/running-your-business/deposits-and-payments)
- **VIC reform in progress:** A government "Clearer and fairer domestic building contracts" review proposes raising the major domestic building contract threshold from $10,000 to $20,000 — [Engage Victoria](https://engage.vic.gov.au/clearer-and-fairer-domestic-building); one commentator says the changes take effect "by March 2027" and move the deposit cap and stage table into regulations — [The Good Builder, secondary](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/deposit-caps-and-stage-claims-differ-in-every-state-victorias-rules-change-by-march-2027/). HIA's Feb 2024 submission to the DBC Act review — [HIA submission](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/-/media/files/newsroom/submissions/2024/hia---dbca-submission-28-february-2024.pdf)
- **QLD:** Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991, Schedule 1B (domestic building contracts); cooling-off is "section 35 of Schedule 1B" — [QBCC: Cooling-off period](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/cooling-off-period). Regulator: QBCC. A February 2026 amendment regulation did not change licensing categories or the licensing threshold — [Boss Lawyers, secondary](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-licensing-changes-2026-what-queensland-builders-and-contractors-need-to-know/)
- **WA:** Home Building Contracts Act 1991 (WA). Regulator: Building and Energy (Dept of Energy, Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety; now on wa.gov.au) — [WA Gov: Building or renovating your home](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home); [WA HBC Act factsheet (Oct 2024)](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/home_building_contracts_act_factsheet.pdf); [WA legislation: HBC Act](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_25408.pdf/$FILE/Home%20Building%20Contracts%20Act%201991%20-%20[04-c0-03].pdf?OpenElement=). Contractor registration is under the Building Services (Registration) Act 2011 — [WA Gov: obligations for registered building services providers](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/information-and-obligations-registered-building-services-providers)
- **SA:** Building Work Contractors Act 1995 (SA), current version 15.1.2026 — [SA legislation: BWC Act (authorised PDF)](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/__legislation/lz/c/a/building%20work%20contractors%20act%201995/current/1995.87.auth.pdf). Regulator: Consumer and Business Services (CBS) — [CBS: Working as a builder in SA](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/working_as_a_builder_in_SA.pdf). BII is administered via the SA Financing Authority (SAFA) — [SAFA: What's New](https://safa.sa.gov.au/building-indemnity-insurance/whats-new)
- **TAS:** Residential Building Work Contracts and Dispute Resolution Act 2016 (Tas). Regulator: Consumer, Building and Occupational Services (CBOS), which has issued a determination under s 14(2) on withdrawal during cooling-off and owner–builder communications — [CBOS: RBWCDR Act page](https://www.cbos.tas.gov.au/?external-uuid=bff85a7c-93aa-4fc9-b1e7-1862ac6ba6e9); [CBOS Residential Building Consumer Guide July 2024](https://www.cbos.tas.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/405038/Residential-Building-Consumer-Guide-July-2024-Final.pdf). Disputes go to TASCAT — [Construction Lawyer Hobart, secondary](https://constructionlawyerhobart.com.au/getting-deposit-back-after-contract-cancellation/)
- **ACT:** Building Act 2004 (statutory warranties, residential building work insurance). Consumer guidance is on the ACT Planning (City and Environment Directorate) site — [ACT Planning: Statutory warranties](https://www.planning.act.gov.au/community/build-or-renovate/before-you-start/building-contracts/statutory-warranties); [HIA: Builders warranty insurance in the ACT, secondary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/dealing-with-contracts/articles/builders-warranty-insurance-in-the-act)
- **NT:** Building Act 1993 (NT); residential building cover is a **Fidelity Fund certificate**, which replaced the Home Building Certification Fund on 1 Jan 2013 — [NT Gov: Fidelity fund certificate](https://nt.gov.au/property/building/build-or-renovate-your-home/residential-building-insurance/fidelity-fund-certificate); [Fidelity Fund NT: How it works](https://www.fidelityfundnt.com.au/how-it-works55b629f0); [NT Law Handbook: Building](https://austlii.community/foswiki/pub/publish/file/NTLawHbk/Building.html)

### Inferences
- A SaaS should key rules on (state, contract signing date), because VIC, SA and NT thresholds changed in the 2025–26 period and NSW's new Act will commence by proclamation.
- In NSW, the regulator name in templates should cover both "NSW Fair Trading" and "Building Commission NSW". Guidance URLs have moved to nsw.gov.au.

### Gaps
- Exact current NSW regulator split (which functions Building Commission NSW vs Fair Trading holds) was not verified from a primary page.
- VIC: the formal renaming of the VBA to the BPC (date, enabling Act) was not verified. The BPC URLs above show the BPC exists and runs Home Warranty.
- NT Building Practitioners Board role and ACT regulator (Access Canberra licensing vs ACT Planning guidance) not verified from primary pages.

## Q2. Does roofing-only work fall under each regime; contract-value thresholds (written contract, licensing, prescribed contents, small vs major)

### Takeaway
Thresholds verified via search: NSW written contract over $5,000 (small job $5k–$20k, large job over $20k); VIC major domestic building contract over $10,000; QLD regulated contracts over $3,300 (level 1/level 2 split at $20,000); WA HBC Act applies to fixed-price contracts $7,500–$500,000; SA contract/insurance threshold reportedly $20,000 from 10 Nov 2025; NT written contract and registration at $12,000. The roofing-specific scope is largely **unverified**. In VIC there is a live question whether roofing-only work is an exempt single-trade job.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW:** Work between $5,000 and $20,000 requires a "small jobs" contract. Work over $20,000 requires a "large jobs" contract — [Fair Trading: Guide to home building contracts](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/construction-and-trade-essentials/guide-to-home-building-contracts) (via search summary); [Point CPD, secondary](https://pointcpd.com.au/blog/nsw-home-building-contracts-rules/)
- **NSW:** Large-jobs contracts must include insurance details, progress payments, warranties and the 5-day cooling-off period — [NSW Gov: Guide to providing home building contracts](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/your-obligations-to-your-customers/guide-to-providing-home-building-contracts)
- **VIC:** Major domestic building contract threshold "currently $10,000"; proposal to raise to $20,000 (not yet law) — [Engage Victoria](https://engage.vic.gov.au/clearer-and-fairer-domestic-building)
- **VIC roofing scope (conflict):** CAV says a major domestic building contract is not required for jobs involving only one listed trade (plumbing, gas-fitting, draining, painting, tiling, electrical, glazing, insulating). CAV's list as summarised does not name roofing — [CAV: What work requires a building contract?](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/builders-and-tradespeople/running-your-business/domestic-building-contracts/what-work-requires-a-building-contract). HIA's summary lists "Plumbing (sanitary, water, gas, roofing, air conditioning, etc.)" as exempt when it is the only work — [HIA, secondary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/managing-compliance/articles/legal-obligations-when-working-for-an-owner-builder-vic). The VBA describes **roof tiling** as its own domestic builder class (covering repair and renovation of existing roofs) — [VBA: What is domestic building work?](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/registration-and-licensing/building-practitioner-registration/domestic-builder/what-is-domestic-building-work). The VBA describes **roofing (stormwater)** as a separate plumbing licence class — [VBA: Roofing stormwater work](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/registration-and-licensing/plumbing-registration-and-licensing/roofing-stormwater)
- **QLD:** Contracts for building work over $3,300 must include advice about the right to withdraw (cooling-off) — [QBCC: Cooling-off period](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/cooling-off-period). Licensing threshold for residential building work unchanged by the Feb 2026 amendment regulation — [Boss Lawyers, secondary](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-licensing-changes-2026-what-queensland-builders-and-contractors-need-to-know/). The QBCC publishes a Level 2 renovation, extension and repair contract template — [QBCC Level 2 template (Dec 2020)](https://qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-09/template-contract-level-2-reno-extend-repair-20.12.pdf) — and a contract checklist (v2 03/25) — [QBCC contract checklist](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/guide-qbcc-contract-checklist.pdf)
- **QLD:** No evidence found of any change to the $3,300 threshold, and no source supports an "$11,000" threshold — [Ilura, secondary](https://iluratechnology.com/keel/queensland-contractor-license-requirements/); [Boss Lawyers, secondary](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-licensing-changes-2026-what-queensland-builders-and-contractors-need-to-know/)
- **WA:** The HBC Act covers fixed-cost contracts between $7,500 and $500,000, whether or not the builder is registered. Outside that range there is no statutory deposit limit — [WA Gov: Building or renovating your home](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home)
- **SA:** Below the threshold there is "no legislative requirement to have a written contract or building indemnity insurance" — [CBS: Working as a builder in SA](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/working_as_a_builder_in_SA.pdf) (via search summary). A secondary source says the $20,000 threshold also applies to written contracts — [prepare.com.au, secondary/unconfirmed](https://prepare.com.au/blog/builders-licence-sa-guide)
- **SA:** CBS's prescribed "Form 1 – Your building contract: your rights and obligations" notice exists for domestic building work contracts — [CBS Form 1](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/form-1-building-contract-rights-and-obligations.pdf)
- **TAS:** The CBOS Residential Building Consumer Guide (July 2024) is the official consumer guide — [CBOS guide](https://www.cbos.tas.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/405038/Residential-Building-Consumer-Guide-July-2024-Final.pdf); for when the Act applies, see [LegalVision, secondary](https://legalvision.com.au/home-building-tasmania/)
- **ACT:** Statutory warranty and insurance requirements apply to residential building work that **requires building approval** and costs **$12,000 or more**. Owner-builder work and Territory/Commonwealth work are excluded — [ACT Planning: Statutory warranties](https://www.planning.act.gov.au/community/build-or-renovate/before-you-start/building-contracts/statutory-warranties)
- **NT:** A written contract is not required if the work is under $12,000 or the builder is an owner-builder. Registration is required for building work of $12,000 or more — [NT Law Handbook: Building](https://austlii.community/foswiki/pub/publish/file/NTLawHbk/Building.html); [prepare.com.au, secondary](https://prepare.com.au/blog/builders-licence-nt-guide)

### Inferences
- **ACT and NT** regimes (warranty/insurance, and in NT fidelity cover) key off work needing **building approval/permit**. Re-roofing like-for-like and repairs often do not need approval, so many roofing jobs in those territories may fall outside the insurance/warranty triggers. Not verified per-job.
- **SA** BII likewise needs council/development approval (see Q5), so roof repairs and restorations may often fall outside BII.
- **VIC:** Roofing-only jobs by a registered **plumber (roofing (stormwater) class)** may be argued to be single-trade exempt "plumbing". Roof tiling/re-roofing by a domestic builder (roof tiling class) is likely domestic building work. Until verified, the SaaS should treat VIC roofing jobs over $10,000 as major domestic building contracts (the conservative default).

### Gaps (unverified, from background knowledge; verify before encoding)
- NSW: licence required for residential building work over $5,000 (labour + materials, incl GST); HBA s 7 (contract formalities), s 7AAA (small jobs), s 7AA (Consumer Building Guide). Section numbers not verified here. Whether roof restoration (cleaning/painting/re-pointing) and gutter replacement is "residential building work" was not verified; it likely is in NSW.
- VIC: Building Act 1993 domestic builder registration threshold (understood to be $10,000) not verified; exact text of DBC Act s 6 exclusions and DBC Regulations exemptions not read.
- QLD: QBCC Act Sch 1B "level 1" ($3,300–<$20,000) vs "level 2" ($20,000+) labels are understood from the QBCC templates but not confirmed from statutory text; whether roof restoration/painting is in a licence class requiring licence above $3,300 not verified.
- WA: whether registration (understood as required for work over $20,000 needing a building permit) applies to roof replacement not verified.
- SA: licensing requirement for roofing trades (SA licenses building work contractors broadly) not verified.
- TAS: the value threshold above which a written contract is mandatory (believed to be $20,000 for most provisions of the 2016 Act) **not verified**; search found no confirmation.

## Q3. Maximum deposit rules

### Takeaway
NSW 10% (both small and large jobs); VIC 10% if price < $20k, 5% if ≥ $20k; QLD 10% if < $20k, 5% if ≥ $20k (20% where off-site work > 50%; official sources partly conflict); WA 6.5% (within $7.5k–$500k only); SA $1,000 if < $20k, 5% if ≥ $20k (official source not fully confirmed); TAS reportedly 10% if < $50k and 5% if ≥ $50k (template only); ACT and NT unclear or no statutory cap found.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW:** 10% maximum deposit applies to both small-jobs and large-jobs contracts. For jobs over $20,000 the contractor may not ask for a deposit or other payment until home building insurance is taken out and the certificate is given to the owner — [Fair Trading: Guide to home building contracts](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/construction-and-trade-essentials/guide-to-home-building-contracts) (via search summary)
- **VIC:** "A builder must not demand or receive a deposit under a domestic building contract of more than 5% of any contract price that is $20 000 or more; 10% of any contract price that is less than $20 000" — [AustLII: DBC Act s 11](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dbca1995275/s11.html); confirmed by [CAV: Deposits and payments](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/building-and-renovating/plan-and-manage-your-building-project/deposits-and-payments)
- **VIC reform:** The proposal would move deposit caps and stage tables into regulations (timing "by March 2027" per commentary; not law) — [The Good Builder, secondary](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/deposit-caps-and-stage-claims-differ-in-every-state-victorias-rules-change-by-march-2027/)
- **QLD (conflict):** The QBCC Consumer Building Guide v3 (effective July 2023) sets the maximum pre-start deposit at 10% where the contract price is less than $20,000, and 20% for any price where off-site work is more than 50% of the contract price — [QBCC Consumer Building Guide v3](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-09/guide-qbcc-consumer-building-v3.pdf). The QBCC Level 2 renovation contract (Dec 2020) refers to a **5%** cap — [QBCC Level 2 template](https://qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-09/template-contract-level-2-reno-extend-repair-20.12.pdf). An unofficial summary says 10% under $20,000 and 5% at $20,000+ — [Scribd copy of guide, secondary](https://www.scribd.com/document/707275457/guide-qbcc-consumer-building-v3)
- **WA:** Maximum deposit is 6.5% of total contract cost (contracts $7,500–$500,000). No statutory limit outside that range — [WA Gov: Building or renovating your home](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home)
- **SA:** For work under $20,000, no deposit over $1,000. For $20,000 or more, the deposit can be no more than 5% of the contract — [LSC SA Law Handbook: Building Work](https://lsc.sa.gov.au/handbook/print/ch23s11.php?lscsa_prod%5Bpage%5D=7); [CBS Form 1](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/form-1-building-contract-rights-and-obligations.pdf) (via search summary; the attribution of each tier to each source was not separable). One guide gives 20% for minor work, which conflicts with the others and is probably outdated (source not identified in summary).
- **TAS:** A plain-language Tasmanian contract template says the deposit must not exceed 5% where the contract price is $50,000 or more, and 10% where it is less than $50,000 — [HIA sample Tasmanian contract, secondary](https://www.uploans.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/HIA-Sample-Building-Contract.pdf)
- **ACT (conflict):** One source says ACT law sets no cap on the deposit (industry practice is about 10%). Another says a builder cannot request more than 10% before commencing on contracts up to $20,000 — sources as returned with [ACT search set: HIA ACT, secondary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/dealing-with-contracts/articles/builders-warranty-insurance-in-the-act); [Rentoule Projects, secondary](https://rentouleprojects.com/blog/choosing-right-builder-canberra/) (the summary did not attribute each claim to a specific URL)
- **NT:** A builder cannot demand a deposit unless Fidelity Fund cover is in place (for prescribed work). One guide says the deposit limit is 5%, which was **not** found in the government source — [HIA: Fidelity Fund insurance NT, secondary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/dealing-with-contracts/articles/what-is-fidelity-fund-insurance); [NT Gov: Fidelity fund certificate](https://nt.gov.au/property/building/build-or-renovate-your-home/residential-building-insurance/fidelity-fund-certificate)

### Inferences
- A safe universal default for the SaaS is **≤5% deposit** when the job is ≥ $20,000. It satisfies VIC, QLD (≥$20k), SA and WA (6.5%). Below $20k: NSW 10%, VIC 10%, QLD 10%, SA capped at $1,000, WA 6.5% (if ≥ $7,500).
- Where insurance is required (NSW > $20k, WA > $20k, SA, VIC, QLD > $3,300, NT, ACT), the deposit should be blocked in-app until the insurance certificate is issued and delivered.

### Gaps
- QLD: exact statutory figures in QBCC Act Sch 1B s 33 not read. Background understanding is 10% (< $20k), 5% (≥ $20k), 20% (off-site > 50%); verify.
- NSW: HBA s 8 (deposit) section number not verified.
- SA: statutory section for the $1,000 / 5% caps not identified, and whether the $20k deposit tier moved with the 10 Nov 2025 BII change is unconfirmed.
- TAS: statutory deposit section not read; template figures only.
- ACT, NT: no primary source found for any statutory deposit cap.

## Q4. Cooling-off periods

### Takeaway
NSW, VIC, QLD, SA and TAS give owners **5 (clear) business days** to withdraw from a home building contract (NSW only for contracts over $20,000; QLD for contracts over $3,300). WA, ACT and NT: no statutory building-contract cooling-off was found. Separately, the ACL 10-business-day termination right applies to unsolicited (door-to-door/phone) agreements in all jurisdictions (see Q8).

### Cited Findings
- **NSW:** Five-business-day cooling-off for large-jobs contracts (over $20,000); it does not apply to small-jobs contracts of $20,000 or less — [Fair Trading guide](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/construction-and-trade-essentials/guide-to-home-building-contracts) (via search summary); [Lawbridge, secondary](https://lawbridge.com.au/insights/nsw-building-contracts-essential-guide/). A secondary source describes it as "five clear business days" — [Point CPD, secondary](https://pointcpd.com.au/blog/nsw-home-building-contracts-rules/). Conflict: [Citilawyers, secondary](https://citilawyers.com.au/articles/home-building-contracts-in-nsw/) says "most" NSW contracts have it (less precise; prefer Fair Trading).
- **QLD:** The owner generally may withdraw within **5 business days** (Sch 1B s 35). The period starts the day after the owner receives the signed contract and, for contracts of $20,000 or more, also the Consumer Building Guide; late delivery of those documents extends the window. Withdrawal is by **written, signed notice citing section 35** — [QBCC: Cooling-off period](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/cooling-off-period)
- **SA:** The owner can cancel by written notice "before the end of 5 clear business days after the making of the contract", subject to the Act's conditions — [LSC SA Law Handbook: Building Work](https://lsc.sa.gov.au/handbook/print/ch23s11.php?lscsa_prod%5Bpage%5D=7) / [CBS Form 1](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/form-1-building-contract-rights-and-obligations.pdf) (via search summary)
- **TAS:** The owner may withdraw within **five business days** of receiving a copy of the signed contract. Exceptions: the owner previously signed a substantially similar contract with the builder, or received formal legal advice before signing — [CBOS Residential Building Consumer Guide July 2024](https://www.cbos.tas.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/405038/Residential-Building-Consumer-Guide-July-2024-Final.pdf). The MBA Tas document points to s 33 — [MBA Tas RBWCDR Act summary, secondary](https://mbatas.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Residential-Building-Work-Contracts-Dispute-Resolution-250518-V2-august.pdf). Conflicting 10-day and 14-day claims come from one law-firm site — [Construction Lawyer Hobart, secondary/unreliable](https://constructionlawyerhobart.com.au/building-contract-cooling-off-period-tasmania/). Written notice is required, and only "legitimate preliminary costs" may be deducted — [Construction Lawyer Hobart, secondary](https://constructionlawyerhobart.com.au/getting-deposit-back-after-contract-cancellation/). ## Q5. Mandatory home warranty / domestic building insurance

### Takeaway
Verified thresholds: NSW HBCF > $20,000 (incl GST); VIC Home Warranty > $20,000 for contracts signed on or after 1 Jul 2026 (DBI > $16,000 before); QLD > $3,300; WA HII > $20,000; SA BII ≥ $20,000 and requires council approval, from 10 Nov 2025 (previously $12,000); ACT ≥ $12,000 work requiring building approval; NT Fidelity Fund certificate for prescribed work, threshold reportedly raised from $12,000 to $25,000 from 30 Mar 2026; TAS has no scheme (not verified here). In every scheme the builder buys the cover, and it must be in place before any deposit is taken or work starts.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW:** Not required if the contract price does not exceed $20,000 incl GST. The builder must provide proof before taking a deposit or starting work. Homeowners can't buy it themselves — [Choice / Contracts Specialist, secondary](https://www.contractsspecialist.com.au/articles/home-warranty-insurance-nsw/); deposit block confirmed by [Fair Trading guide](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/construction-and-trade-essentials/guide-to-home-building-contracts). The new icare HBCF Eligibility Manual took effect 2 Mar 2026; the Building Contract Review Program was retired 2 Mar 2026 (transition to 2 Sep 2026) — [Gianta HBCF 2026, secondary](https://www.gianta.com.au/blog/build-11/nsw-home-building-compensation-fund-hbcf-2026-what-homeowners-and-builders-must-know-about-mandatory-insurance-118); [Feasly, secondary](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/home-warranty-insurance-nsw-developer-guide)
- **VIC:** Home Warranty (provided by the BPC) starts 1 July 2026 for eligible domestic building work valued at **more than $20,000** under contracts signed on or after that date. DBI certificates issued before 1 Jul 2026 continue — [BPC: Home Warranty](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/insurance-for-domestic-building-work/home-warranty); [BPC: DBI and Home Warranty](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/insurance-for-domestic-building-work/domestic-building-insurance-and-home-warranty); [BPC news 2026](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/news/2026/home-warranty-insurance-coming-soon). It is "first resort" cover (incomplete, defective or non-compliant work where the builder is unable or unwilling to fix it). Maximum $400,000 per home. Previous DBI threshold $16,000 — [BPC Home Warranty](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/insurance-for-domestic-building-work/home-warranty); [Choice, secondary](https://www.choice.com.au/money/insurance/home-and-contents/articles/home-warranty-insurance). Builders reportedly must obtain cover within 10 days of signing or before work starts, whichever is first. Eligible work includes repairs, alterations and extensions — [SPASA, secondary](https://www.spasa.com.au/news/vic-home-warranty-insurance); [Lexology, secondary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=9f53f7ff-f167-40c1-9a77-fdc46ba9b230). Conflict: sources differ on whether the trigger is contract price (incl GST) or value of work — [Span28, secondary](https://span28.com.au/span28-article-vic-domestic-building-insurance.html)
- **QLD:** All contractors performing insurable residential construction work with a contract price of more than $3,300 (incl GST) must pay a premium under the Queensland Home Warranty Scheme — [QBCC: Key information for licensees and homeowners](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/guide-key-information-licensee-home-owners.pdf) (via search summary). The government has a stated review of the insurance threshold, cover amount and timeframes (no enacted change) — [Boss Lawyers, secondary](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-licensing-changes-2026-what-queensland-builders-and-contractors-need-to-know/). One guide lists cover as $200,000 per category of loss ($300,000 with optional extra cover) — [Empire Legal, secondary](https://empirelegal.com.au/blog/qbcc-home-warranty-scheme/)
- **WA:** HII is required for residential building work valued over $20,000. The builder must take out the policy **in the owner's name before accepting any payment (including a deposit) or commencing work**. Deposit loss cover up to $40,000 per the official page, versus $20,000 per a third-party site — [WA Gov: Building or renovating your home](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home); [Better Builds, secondary](https://betterbuilds.com.au/wa/how-much-deposit-can-a-builder-ask-for/)
- **SA:** The BII threshold increased to **$20,000** in the Regulations from **10 November 2025** (previously $12,000 or more). The $250,000 policy limit is prescribed in the Building Work Contractors Regulations for all new policies from 10 Nov 2025 — [SAFA: What's New](https://safa.sa.gov.au/building-indemnity-insurance/whats-new); [SAFA: BII](https://safa.sa.gov.au/insurance/building-indemnity-insurance). BII applies where the work needs approval under the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 — [LegalVision SA, secondary](https://legalvision.com.au/home-building-sa/); [CBS: Working as a builder](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/working_as_a_builder_in_SA.pdf). The builder must give the owner a certificate of insurance before taking any money (including the deposit) — [Viabuild, secondary](https://www.viabuild.au/guides/sa-building-indemnity-insurance). Sources are inconsistent on "more than $20,000" vs "$20,000 or more" and on whether GST is included — [SPASA, secondary](https://www.spasa.com.au/news/sa-changes-to-building-indemnity-insurance)
- **ACT:** Residential building work insurance (or a Master Builders ACT Fidelity Fund certificate) is required for work needing building approval costing $12,000 or more, before work starts. Reportedly the policy or certificate must be in place before taking a deposit, signing the head contract or bringing materials on site. Lost-deposit cover is capped at $10,000. The minimum insured amount rose from $85,000 to $200,000 from 1 Jan 2025 (reported; older ACT page says $85,000) — [ACT Planning: Statutory warranties](https://www.planning.act.gov.au/community/build-or-renovate/before-you-start/building-contracts/statutory-warranties); [HIA ACT, secondary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/dealing-with-contracts/articles/builders-warranty-insurance-in-the-act); [Kerin Benson fact sheet, secondary/old](https://kerinbensonlawyers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Kerin-Benson-Lawyers-ACT-Fact-Sheet-4-Statutory-Warranties-and-Residential-Building-Insurance.pdf)
- **NT:** A Fidelity Fund certificate is required for prescribed residential building work (new houses, duplexes, units up to 3 storeys). It covers the builder's death, disappearance, insolvency or loss of registration. The NT Gov page states a minimum prescribed value "from $12,000 to $25,000". A 2026 developer guide says the threshold is over $25,000 from 30 Mar 2026; other sources still cite $12,000 — [NT Gov: Fidelity fund certificate](https://nt.gov.au/property/building/build-or-renovate-your-home/residential-building-insurance/fidelity-fund-certificate); [Feasly, secondary](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/builder-warranty-insurance-australia-by-state)
- **TAS:** No source on Tasmanian home warranty insurance was retrieved in this session.

### Inferences
- For roofing, the regimes that turn on **building approval** (SA BII, ACT, and NT where prescribed work is mostly new dwellings or floor-area increases) are unlikely to catch like-for-like re-roofs and repairs. NSW, VIC (> $20k post 1 Jul 2026), QLD (> $3,300) and WA (> $20k) are value-triggered and more likely to catch large re-roofs. Whether each scheme's eligible/insurable work list includes roofing-only work was **not verified**.

## Q8. Door-to-door / unsolicited sales (ACL) and interaction with building contracts

### Takeaway
The Australian Consumer Law (Sch 2, Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth)) applies uniformly in all eight jurisdictions. For unsolicited consumer agreements, the consumer has a **10 business day** termination period. The supplier must not supply services or take payment during it (s 86), and the period extends (3 or 6 months) if disclosure rules are breached. The ACCC began a review of unsolicited selling and lead generation in June 2025.

### Cited Findings
- 10 business day cooling-off period in which the consumer can cancel for any reason without penalty or payment being taken — [ACCC media release (HICC)](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/hicc-failed-to-tell-consumers-of-cooling-off-period-in-unsolicited-sales-of-private-health-insurance)
- A supplier must not supply goods or services or accept or require any payment during the 10-day cooling-off period (ACL s 86) — [SA Law Handbook: Unsolicited consumer agreements](https://www.lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch10s04s07s02.php)
- If the supplier fails to give required information, the consumer may terminate within **six months**; another source cites **3 months** under s 82(3) for some breaches — [SA Law Handbook](https://www.lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch10s04s07s02.php); [Fitzroy Legal Service Law Handbook](https://fls.org.au/law-handbook-temp/consumers-contracts-the-internet-and-copyright/consumer-protection-laws/unsolicited-sales-practices/)
- Goods (not services) may be supplied within the 10-day period if the total price for goods is $500 or less (reg 95 Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010) — [SA Law Handbook](https://www.lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch10s04s07s02.php) (via search summary)
- ACCC June 2025 consultation paper "Unsolicited selling and lead generation" (review of current rules; possible reforms) — [ACCC consultation paper June 2025](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/public-registers/documents/ACCC%20consultation%20paper%20-%20Unsolicited%20selling%20and%20lead%20generation%20-%20June%202025.pdf?amp=&download=y); [Bird & Bird, secondary](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/burying-the-lead-generation); [ACCC: misleading and high pressure unsolicited sales practices widespread](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/misleading-and-high-pressure-unsolicited-sales-practices-widespread)
- Official ACL guide "Sales practices: a guide for businesses and legal practitioners" — [consumer.gov.au Sales Practices guide](https://consumer.gov.au/sites/consumer/files/2016/05/0553FT_ACL-guides_SalesPractices_web.pdf)

### Inferences
- A roofer canvassing door-to-door (common after storms) creates an unsolicited consumer agreement. For those leads the SaaS should: (a) block job scheduling and payment capture for 10 business days after the agreement; (b) attach the ACL termination notice/form; (c) run the state building-contract cooling-off at the same time (5 business days in NSW/VIC/QLD/SA/TAS), with the longer ACL period controlling.
- The deposit rules in Q3 cannot be used to collect money inside the ACL 10-business-day window for unsolicited sales.

### Gaps (background knowledge, verify)
- ACL s 69 definition (negotiated by phone or away from business premises, not invited by the consumer, total price over $100 or not ascertainable). Not verified this session.
- ACL s 73 permitted calling hours: 9am–6pm weekdays, 9am–5pm Saturday, no calls Sundays or public holidays. The search did **not** confirm these hours.
- ACL s 74 (disclose purpose/identity), s 75 (leave on request), s 76 (inform of termination right), s 79 (copy of agreement, termination form). Not verified.
- Any urgent-repairs exemption (e.g. consumer-requested emergency repairs) **not verified**; the "$100 urgent repairs" premise in the brief is unsupported.
- How each state's building Act interacts with ACL (e.g. whether state cooling-off and ACL termination both apply, or a state statute carves out) was not researched successfully.
