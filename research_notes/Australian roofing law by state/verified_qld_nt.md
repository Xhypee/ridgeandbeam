## QLD — VERIFIED RULES

### 1. Governing Act and regulator — VERIFIED
- **Value:** Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991. Domestic building contracts are regulated by **Schedule 1B** (s 67AZN). The regulator is the **Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC)**. The Act is supported by the QBCC Regulation 2018.
- **Quote:** "Domestic building contracts are regulated under schedule 1B." (s 67AZN(1)) — [QBCC Act]
- **2025 changes:**
  - The Planning (Social Impact and Community Benefit) and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2025 (2025 No. 14) replaced "in a written form" (defined as handwritten or typewritten) with "**in writing**" in Sch 1B ss 13–14. This supports electronic contracts. Compare the 2024-07-01 version, which read "must be in a written form, dated and signed".
  - The same Act added s 67WBA. Under it, an informal "arrangement … reached through an exchange of emails" counts as a contract for home warranty purposes.

### 2. Does roofing-only work fall under the regime? — VERIFIED
- **Value:** Yes. "Domestic building work" includes "the renovation, alteration, extension, improvement or repair of a home" (Sch 1B s 4(1)(b)). A re-roof, roof repair, restoration or gutter job on a home is therefore domestic building work.
- **No single-trade exemption.** The only exclusions are:
  - "excluded building work": farm, business-only or animal buildings, design work, plans, foundations data, transport (Sch 1B s 1);
  - non-homes listed in Reg s 46 (e.g. guesthouse, motel, hospital, caravan);
  - contracts with subcontractors, with owner-builders, or for 2+ detached dwellings (Sch 1B s 3(2)).
- **Regulated contracts:** Sch 1B applies its main rules (contract form, deposits, cooling-off, warranties) only to "regulated contracts", i.e. a contract price **more than $3,300** (Sch 1B s 5).
- **Roof cleaning:** cleaning alone is not building work. QBCC A–Z guide (July 2024): "Note: Cleaning of a roof is not building work and therefore, in isolation, is not insurable work."
- **Scaffolding:** "Erection of scaffolding" is not building work (QBCC Reg sch 1 s 26).
- **Quote:** "regulated amount means $3,300 or the higher amount, if any, prescribed by a regulation." (Sch 1B s 1) No higher amount is prescribed in the Reg (whole Reg searched). — [QBCC Act], [QBCC Reg]; QBCC A–Z guide https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-03/guide-insurable-work-a-to-z.pdf

### 3. Licence classes, threshold, licence number display — VERIFIED (one nuance CORRECTED)

**Licence classes (QBCC Reg sch 2):**
- **Part 43 "Roof and wall cladding" scope:**
  - "Select and install roof cladding other than terracotta and concrete roof tiles"
  - "Fabricate and install rainwater goods"
  - flash penetrations (skylights, ventilators)
  - external flashings, metal ceilings, soffits and fascias
  - "Design roof drainage components"
  - "Refurbish and maintain roofs, **excluding painting roofs**"
  - incidental work
- **Part 58 "Roof tiling" scope:**
  - "Cut and fix roof tiles, including tiles of concrete, clay, metal or similar material, shingles and shakes"
  - battens; bedding and pointing; flashing; safety mesh, sarking and anti-ponding boards; skylights
  - "Refurbish and maintain roofs, excluding painting roofs"
  - incidental work
- **CORRECTION / new detail — roof painting:** roof painting is **excluded** from both roofing classes. It falls under **Part 44 "Painting and decorating"** ("apply paint or other substance for protective, decorative or technical purposes"). A roof restoration that includes painting over $3,300 therefore needs a painting and decorating licence for the painting part, unless that part is "incidental work" of the roofing class.
- **Incidental work:** Reg s 7 lets a licensee carry out (or engage a licensee to carry out) incidental work of another class "with a total value of $3,300 or less".

**Threshold:**
- **Value:** a licence is required for building work valued **over $3,300**.
- **Quote:** "Work of a value of $3,300 or less" is not building work (Reg sch 1 s 2). The exceptions listed are: fire protection; schedule 2 parts 11–14 (building design, completed residential building inspection), 18 (plumbing and drainage), 19 (drainage), 36 (gasfitting), 51 (site classifier) and 56 (termite – chemical); part 38 (hydraulic design) over $1,100; and mechanical services. **Roofing classes (parts 43 and 58) are not exceptions, so the $3,300 threshold applies to them.**
- **QBCC wording:** "individuals and companies must hold a QBCC licence to carry out building work that is: valued over $3,300 …" ("Who must hold a QBCC licence", last reviewed 16 Apr 2026). QBCC says the value includes labour, materials and GST ("Contracts and agreement types" page).
- **Licence number display:**
  - **Contracts:** both level 1 and level 2 regulated contracts must contain "the building contractor's licence number as it appears on the building contractor's licence" (Sch 1B ss 13(3)(b), 14(3)(b)).
  - **Advertisements:** Act s 54 says a licensee's advertisement must "contain the name under which the licensee is licensed" and "state that the licensee is licensed under this Act and the number of the licensee's licence". Maximum penalty 35 penalty units.
  - **Unlicensed person's advertisement** must "state the value of the work the person is entitled to carry out without a licence" (s 53C).
  - **Quotes:** no express statutory rule requiring the licence number on a quote was found. A quote that is published as advertising, or that becomes the contract, would be caught. Status for quotes: **UNVERIFIED (no express rule found); recommend showing it.**
- **URLs:** [QBCC Reg]; [QBCC Act]; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/finding-contractor-getting-quotes/who-must-hold-qbcc-licence

### 4. Written contract thresholds and tiers — VERIFIED
- **Tiers:**
  - **≤ $3,300:** no Sch 1B contract required. QBCC: "A contract is not required if the work is valued less than $3,300 (including labour, materials and GST)." QBCC provides a "small building projects contract" template.
  - **Level 1:** more than $3,300 and less than $20,000 (Sch 1B s 6).
  - **Level 2:** $20,000 or more. Reg s 45: "the prescribed amount is $20,000."
- **Both levels:** the contract "must be in writing and dated and signed by or on behalf of each of the parties to it". It "has effect only if it complies with subsection (2)" (ss 13(2),(5); 14(2),(10)).
- **Level 1 contents:**
  - party names, with the contractor's name as on the licence
  - licence number
  - description of the work
  - plans and specifications
  - price or method of calculating it
  - date for practical completion
  - "a conspicuous notice advising the building owner of the right the owner may have to withdraw from the contract under schedule section 35"
- **Level 2 adds:**
  - start date
  - statement of each statutory warranty
  - a fixed price "stated in a prominent position on the first page of the contract schedule"
  - a price-change warning and explanation on the first page of the schedule
  - no misleading contractor name (s 14(8))
- **Copies and notices:**
  - Copy to the owner within **5 business days** (s 15).
  - Level 2 commencement notice within **10 business days** of starting work (s 16).
  - The contractor must not start work before the contract complies (s 30; 100 penalty units).
- **Contract price:** "the total amount payable under the contract" (s 2). QBCC states the values include GST.
- **URLs:** [QBCC Act]; [QBCC Reg]; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/contracts-agreement-types (last reviewed 27 Jun 2025)

### 5. Maximum deposit — VERIFIED (round-1 "conflict" RESOLVED)
- **Value (Sch 1B s 33(1)):** "must not, before starting to provide the contracted services at the building site, demand or receive a deposit … of more than":
  - (a) level 1: **10%** of the contract price;
  - (b) level 2: **5%**;
  - (c) level 1 or 2 where "the value of the off-site work is more than 50% of the contract price": **20%**.
- **What counts as a deposit:** "any payment for domestic building work demanded or received by the building contractor prior to the contracted services commencing at the building site, **including … any insurance premium**" (s 33(3)). The QHWS premium therefore counts toward the cap.
- **Penalty:** 100 penalty units.
- **Round-1 conflict resolved:** the 5% / 10% / 20% figures are all statutory, and they are cumulative tiers, not alternatives.
- **Jobs ≤ $3,300:** there is no statutory cap. QBCC guidance says "The maximum deposit for works valued at $3,300 or less is 20%". This is QBCC guidance only; no statutory provision was found.
- **Wording inconsistency:** the QBCC page says level 2 is "more than $20,000", but the statute says "equal to or more than" $20,000. **Use the statute.**
- **URLs:** [QBCC Act]; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/deposits-progress-payments (last reviewed 27 Jun 2025)

### 6. Cooling-off — VERIFIED
- **Length and trigger:** "may withdraw from the contract within **5 business days** after the day on which the owner receives a copy of the signed contract from the building contractor" (Sch 1B s 35(1)).
  - Level 2: if the consumer building guide is received after the signed contract, the 5 business days run from receipt of the guide (s 35(2)).
  - If neither document is received within 5 business days of contract, the owner may still withdraw (s 35(3)).
- **Threshold:** regulated contracts only, i.e. more than $3,300.
- **Exceptions (s 36):**
  - a previous regulated contract on substantially the same terms, for substantially the same services and the same home or land;
  - the owner received paid "formal legal advice" before entering the contract, or tells the contractor so.
- **Waiver (s 39):** "The building owner under a repair contract may waive the building owner's right to withdraw from the contract." This is done by signed written waiver notice. A repair contract includes "an alteration, improvement or replacement that it is necessary or reasonable to carry out instead of effecting a repair". **This is very relevant to storm or leak repairs.**
- **Withdrawal notice (s 37):** "a written notice signed by the building owner … stating— (a) that the building owner withdraws from the contract; and (b) the section of this schedule under which the withdrawal is made."
- **What the contractor keeps (s 38):** out-of-pocket expenses reasonably incurred, **plus $100** if the owner withdraws under s 35(1). Any balance of the prepaid amount is refunded.
- **Prescribed notice wording:** **none exists.** The Act requires only a "conspicuous notice" (ss 13(3)(g), 14(3)(i)), and the Reg prescribes no wording (Reg Part 7, ss 45–47, read in full). Status: VERIFIED that there is no prescribed text. Suggested in-app text, paraphrasing the QBCC page: "Under section 35 of Schedule 1B of the QBCC Act, you generally have the right to withdraw from the contract during a cooling-off period of five (5) business days."
- **URLs:** [QBCC Act]; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/contracts-payments/cooling-off-period (last reviewed 27 Jun 2025)

### 7. Home warranty — Queensland Home Warranty Scheme (QHWS) — VERIFIED (roofing coverage now confirmed)
- **Scheme:** the statutory insurance scheme in Act Part 5, administered by the QBCC.
- **Threshold:** primary insurable work "if carried out by a licensed contractor and the insurable value of the work is **more than the regulated amount**" ($3,300) (s 67WC).
  - QBCC: "compulsory for all residential construction work valued at more than $3,300 (including cost of materials, labour and GST)".
  - Insurable value = "the reasonable cost to the insurer of having the work carried out … on the basis that all building and other materials are to be supplied by the contractor". **A labour-only quote must therefore be valued including materials.**
- **Roofing-only covered? YES:**
  - "building work within the **building envelope**". The example of a building envelope given is "the slab and footings system, an external wall **and a roof**" (s 67WC(1)(b), (3)).
  - Reg s 27(a) adds "renovation, alteration, extension, improvement or repair of the building envelope". Reg s 27(c)(iii) adds stormwater discharge structures, with the example "repairing a downpipe or gutter that drains stormwater".
  - QBCC A–Z guide (July 2024) entries for "Roof restorations (including painting)", "Guttering", "Gutter-guard", "Skylights" and "Fascias" each state: "if over $3,300 IS primary insurable work: premium is payable on the whole contract value for the works".
- **Duplexes and multi-unit buildings (≤ 3 storeys):** roof and guttering work is valued per living unit only where its insurable value is more than $5,000 (Reg ss 34(3)(a), 35(3)(a)).
- **Excluded:** owner-builder work; commercial premises; buildings over 3 storeys (s 67WB, 67WE).
- **When the premium is paid:** the licensed contractor "must collect from the consumer, and pay to the commission, the appropriate insurance premium before the first of the following to happen— (a) **10 business days** elapse from the day the contract was entered into; (b) the residential construction work starts." (s 68B(2); 100 penalty units)
  - QBCC issues a "Notice of Cover" to the owner by email. The Consumer Building Guide (Oct 2026) says the owner "should receive a Notice of Cover within two weeks of signing your contract".
  - **The SaaS should prompt premium payment before the earlier of those two dates, and log the Notice of Cover.**
- **Cover:**
  - $200,000 maximum. Optional cover up to $300,000 must be bought within 30 business days of the contract or before work starts (Act s 67Z; Consumer Building Guide).
  - Cover lasts 6 years 6 months (QBCC page "What is home warranty insurance", last reviewed 11 May 2025). **This explains round-1's "6 years 6 months" figure: it is the QHWS cover period, not the statutory warranty.**
  - Cost-plus contracts are covered for defects only.
- **URLs:** [QBCC Act]; [QBCC Reg]; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/queensland-home-warranty-scheme/what-home-warranty-insurance ; https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/queensland-home-warranty-scheme/who-pays-premium ; A–Z guide PDF above

### 8. Documents, progress payments, variations — VERIFIED, with one CORRECTION (new guide version)

**Consumer Building Guide (level 2, $20,000 or more):**
- **Rule:** "must give the building owner a copy of the consumer building guide **before the owner signs** the contract" (Sch 1B s 18; 20 penalty units).
- **CORRECTED — new version:** the guide at the QBCC URL is now a new version, "CONSUMER BUILDING GUIDE FOR HOME OWNERS … EFFECTIVE OCTOBER 2026", doc code **FSG0116_v2_10/26** (PDF created 19 Oct 2026). Round 1 said "v3, effective July 2023". The QBCC resource page is still titled "v3" but serves the October 2026 file.
- **New acknowledgement form:** the new guide includes a "Home Owners Acknowledgement of Guide" checklist with name, signature and date, "return to your building contractor. This guide will be retained as part of their records". **The SaaS should capture the signed acknowledgement before the owner signs the contract.**
- **URL:** https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-09/guide-qbcc-consumer-building-v3.pdf

**Other documents:**
- signed copy of the contract within 5 business days (s 15);
- level 2 commencement notice within 10 business days of starting on site (s 16);
- certificates of inspection where the contractor engaged the certifier (s 17);
- statutory warranty statement in level 2 contracts (s 14(3)(h));
- QHWS Notice of Cover (QBCC);
- defects document at practical completion (Sch 1B s 1, "practical completion");
- QBCC Contract Checklist (resource /resources/guide/qbcc-contract-checklist). The checklist was not re-read this round.

**Progress payments (s 34):**
- **Rule:** may claim only if the amount "(a) is directly related to the progress of carrying out the subject work at the building site; and (b) is proportionate to the value of the subject work that relates to the claim, or less than that value." (50 penalty units)
- **Example given in the Act:** half the contract price less a 5% deposit, claimed after half the work is done.

**Variations (s 40):**
- The variation must be in writing and given to the owner before the earlier of 5 business days after agreement or the start of the varied work. There is an urgent-work defence.
- "must not start to carry out any domestic building work the subject of the variation before the building owner agrees to the variation in writing".
- **s 41 contents:** description, date of request, delay estimate, price change or method, and when the increase is payable. "Any increase in the contract price as a result of the variation can not be required to be paid before work the subject of the variation is started."

**Other rules:**
- **Extension of time (s 42):** the claim must be in writing within 10 business days of the contractor becoming aware of the delay, and the owner must approve it in writing.
- **Caveats (s 43):** the contractor gets no interest in a resident owner's land, and must not lodge a caveat (100 penalty units).
- **Arbitration clauses (s 32):** void.
- **URLs:** [QBCC Act]; QBCC Consumer Building Guide PDF (above)

### 9. Statutory warranties — VERIFIED (round-1 "6 yrs 6 months" CLARIFIED)
- **Implied warranties (Sch 1B ss 20–26):** part of every regulated contract, i.e. more than $3,300:
  - materials good, suitable and new
  - compliance with laws
  - appropriate, skilful work with reasonable care
  - follows plans and specifications
  - suitable for occupation
  - reasonable diligence
  - provisional sums and prime cost items calculated with care
- **Warranty period (s 29(3)):** "is **6 years** for a breach that results in a structural defect … or **1 year** in any other case". It starts on completion, termination, or the date work ceased.
- **Late extension (s 29(2)):** "if the breach of statutory warranty becomes apparent within the last 6 months of the warranty period, proceedings may be started within a further 6 months after the end of the warranty period."
- **Warranties run with the building** (s 27). **Contracting out is void** (s 28).
- The separate QHWS cover runs 6 years 6 months (see item 7).
- **URL:** [QBCC Act]

### 10. Post-job plumbing compliance / notice of work — VERIFIED, with one CORRECTION (scope)
- **Roof stormwater is not regulated plumbing or drainage in QLD.** Under the Plumbing and Drainage Act 2018 dictionary:
  - "drainage" covers only pipes carrying **sewage**, effluent or greywater, plus on-site sewage facilities;
  - "plumbing" covers water supply "from a water service provider's infrastructure or a water storage tank", water within premises, and above-ground sewage pipes.
  - Gutters and downpipes are building work ("Fabricate and install rainwater goods", Roof and wall cladding licence).
- **CORRECTED:** round 1 suggested a Form 4 may be needed for a "stormwater connection". **Stormwater drainage is not "drainage" under the P&D Act**, so a Form 4 is not triggered by gutters, downpipes or stormwater work alone.
- **When Form 4 is triggered:** only if the roofer does plumbing work, e.g. connecting a rainwater tank to supply water into the house, done by a licensed plumber.
- **Notifiable work deadlines (P&D Act s 83):**
  - "within **10 business days** after notifiable work is finished, the relevant person … must give … (a) a notice, in the approved form, about the work; (b) the fee prescribed by regulation" (60 penalty units). The approved form is QBCC Form 4.
  - A copy and an explanatory statement go to the occupier or customer within **20 business days** (s 83(3)).
  - No dollar threshold applies.
- **URL:** [P&D Act]

### 11. Building approval for re-roofing — VERIFIED (interpretation flagged)
- **Value:** Building Regulation 2021 sch 1 lists accepted development (self-assessable), which needs no building approval but must still comply with building assessment provisions such as the NCC and wind tie-down.
  - **s 7:** "repairs, maintenance or alterations to an existing building" that do not "(a) change the building or structure's floor area or height; or (b) affect a structural component … or (c) affect the fire safety system". The exception carved out of s 7 is "repairs, maintenance or alterations to an existing solar hot water system or photovoltaic solar panel attached to the roof".
  - **s 8:** allows work affecting only a "minor structural component", e.g. "a roof beam or lintel supporting no more than 5m² of roof area", and work affecting no more than 20% of components of the same type.
  - **s 4(1)(a):** the cladding carve-out excludes "the roof", so roof re-cladding is not removed from accepted development.
- **Inference (not a stated rule):**
  - A like-for-like re-sheet or re-tile with no change to height or structure → accepted development, no approval needed.
  - Replacing more than 20% of battens or rafters, changing roof height or pitch, or touching PV/solar → building approval through a private certifier is likely needed.
  - No QLD-specific "re-roofing" provision or cyclone tie-down trigger exists in the Building Regulation (searched for "re-roof", "roof covering", "tie-down"); NCC compliance still applies.
- **URL:** [Bldg Reg]

### 12. Security of payment — VERIFIED (round-1 disagreement RESOLVED)
- **Act:** Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017, Chapter 3 "Progress payments".
- **Resident-owner exclusion:** s 61(2)(b): "This chapter does not apply to— … (b) a construction contract for the carrying out of domestic building work if a resident owner is a party to the contract, to the extent the contract relates to a building or part of a building where the resident owner resides or intends to reside".
  - The exclusion covers **all of Chapter 3** (payment claims, schedules and adjudication), not just adjudication.
  - "Resident owner" takes the QBCC Act meaning: an individual who intends to live there on completion or within 6 months.
- **Effect for roofers:**
  - **Owner-occupier: no.**
  - Landlords, companies, strata bodies and builders: yes.
  - Subcontract to a builder: yes.
- **Due date:** if the contract is silent, "10 business days after the day a payment claim … is made" (s 73(1)(b)).
- **Payment schedule:** due within the contract period or "15 business days after the payment claim is given", whichever ends first (s 76(1)). If no schedule is given, the respondent is liable for the full claimed amount (s 77).
- **URL:** [BIF Act]

### 13. Other prompts (QLD) — VERIFIED
- **Falls (WHS Reg Qld s 306D):** QLD-specific thresholds. Control measures (edge protection, travel restraint, fall arrest) are required before work starts where:
  - (a) **housing construction work** with a possible fall of at least **3m**;
  - (b) other construction work with a possible fall of at least **2m**;
  - (c) "construction work on a roof … with a slope over 26º" at **any height**.
- **Re-roofing usually gets the 2m threshold.** "Housing construction work" means work "to erect, construct, extend or structurally alter" a detached house and similar dwellings. A non-structural re-roof or repair of an existing house is therefore **not** housing construction work, so the **2m** threshold applies, not 3m. (Inference from the s 306A definition; flag for legal review.)
- **Edge protection specs (s 306E):** top rail at least 900mm above the surface; bottom rail 150–250mm high.
- **Ladders (ss 306K–306M):** industrial ladders rated 120kg; single ladders up to 6.1m and extension ladders up to 7.5m; 3 points of contact where the possible fall is 2m or more (3m for housing).
- **SWMS:** "high risk construction work" includes "a risk of a person falling more than 2m" and "disturbance of asbestos" (s 291). A SWMS is required.
- **Asbestos:**
  - A licensed removalist is required unless the job is "10m² or less of non-friable asbestos" (s 458(2)). Smaller jobs need a competent, trained person (s 458(3)).
  - A licensed removalist must notify the regulator (WHSQ) "at least 5 days before" licensed removal work (s 466).
  - Asbestos removal is not building work and is not insurable on its own (QBCC A–Z guide).
- **Liens:** no caveat on a resident owner's land (Sch 1B s 43).
- **Contract enforceability:** a contract that does not meet the written, dated and signed requirement "has effect only if" it complies. **The app should block "start job" until both parties have signed and the contract is dated** (ss 13(5), 14(10), 30).
- **URLs:** [WHS Reg Qld]; [QBCC Act]

### 1. Governing Act and regulator — VERIFIED (partly)

Those parts include contractor rules (Pt 4A), Part 5A and permits (Pt 6). **Their practical reach is the gazetted "building control areas".** The list of areas was not read (nt.gov.au blocked).

### 2. Does roofing-only work fall under the residential regime? — VERIFIED (key finding)
- **Value: generally NO for re-roofing an existing house.** The contractor-registration, written-contract and insurance regimes all exclude renovations that do not increase floor area. Each regulation gives **"The re-cladding of the roof … of a detached house"** as its example of excluded work:
  - **Registration** (s 48A): BR r 41A(2)(a) excludes "renovations or alterations to an existing Class 1a or Class 2 building if there is no increase in the floor area of the building". Example 1: "The re-cladding of the roof or walls of a Class 1a or Class 2 building."
  - **Written contract** (s 48B): BR r 41G(2)(a) has the same exclusion for Class 1a. Example: "The re-cladding of the roof or walls of a detached house."
  - **RBI / fidelity cover and Part 5A consumer guarantees:** RBI r 5(5)(b) excludes "renovations or alterations to an existing relevant building that do not increase the floor area". Examples include "The re-cladding of roofs or walls".
- **Commercial and other building classes (1b, 3–9):** renovations are excluded from registration only if floor area, height, structural support elements and use are all unchanged (r 41A(2)(ab)).
- **Roof work on a new house or extension:** roofing done as part of building a new Class 1a house or an extension falls under the head builder's regime. The roofer is then a subcontractor.
- **URLs:** [NT BR]; [NT RBI]

### 3. Licence / registration — VERIFIED (round-1 gap CLOSED)
- **Categories:** "building contractor" sub-categories are residential (restricted), residential (unrestricted), commercial (restricted) and commercial (unrestricted) (BR rr 41B–41CB; Act s 4A). **There is no roofing-specific category.**
- **When registration is needed:** a building contractor must be registered for "prescribed building work" (Act s 48A; 85 penalty units). Re-roofing an existing house is not prescribed building work (item 2), so **an NT roofer re-roofing an existing house does not need building contractor registration under s 48A.**
- **Dollar threshold — CORRECTED:**
  - **Value:** **$25,000** for Class 1a, 2 or 10 work (BR r 41E), from 30 Mar 2026.
  - **Old value:** $12,000. [NT AMD26] r 15: "Regulation 41E omit $12 000 insert $25 000". The same change applies to r 41J (contract) and r 41P (owner-builder).
  - **Transitional rule:** BR r 57 keeps the old $12,000 for "building work that commenced or was carried out before the commencement".
- **Plumbing:**
  - "plumbing" means fixtures, fittings and pipes "designed to receive and carry sewage or water", plus water services. "draining" means sub-surface pipes to sewer, drain or septic tank ([NT PDL] s 4).
  - Whether roof gutters and downpipes are "plumbing" in the NT is **UNVERIFIED**. Round 1 cited a Board statement that "roof plumbing" is not an NT licence category, but that page (plumberslicensing.nt.gov.au) was blocked this round.
- **Licence number display:**
  - Contracts: for prescribed residential contracts, "specifying the prescribed building contractor's registration number" (BR r 41H(c)).
  - Display: the registration certificate must be shown "in a conspicuous place at the building practitioner's principal place of business" (Act s 37).
  - Corporations: must ensure the registered nominee's name "appears in all advertisements published by the corporation relating to the function" (s 25).
  - **No statutory rule found requiring a number on quotes or ads for non-prescribed (re-roof) work.**
- **URLs:** [NT BA]; [NT BR]; [NT AMD26]; [NT PDL]

### 4. Written contract thresholds — VERIFIED / CORRECTED
- **Rule:** a written contract with prescribed contents is required only for **prescribed building work**, by a residential building contractor, valued at **$25,000 or more** (Act s 48B(3)(a); BR r 41J "the prescribed amount is $25 000").
  - Prescribed building work here means construction of a new Class 1a detached house, plus attached Class 10 built at the same time, or a supporting retaining wall (BR r 41G).
  - **CORRECTED:** round 1 said $12,000. That was the value before 30 Mar 2026.
- **Re-roofing:** excluded (item 2). **No statutory written-contract requirement** applies to NT re-roofing, repair or gutter jobs.
  - ACL unsolicited-agreement rules still apply. Best practice is to use a written contract.
- **Contents where it applies (r 41H):** single project; contractor identity; registration number; extent of work and total price; deposit (max 5%); stage percentages; dispute resolution.
  - A contract that "does not comply with regulation 41H" is an offence (r 41HD; 100 penalty units).
  - Arbitration or expert-determination clauses requiring a final and binding decision are of no effect (r 41HC).
- **URLs:** [NT BA]; [NT BR]

### 5. Maximum deposit — VERIFIED (round-1 "5% not found in government source" now FOUND)
- **Value:** **5%** of the total contracted price (BR r 41HE(1); 50 penalty units). This applies only to s 48B residential building contracts, i.e. new houses of $25,000 or more.
- **Payment rules for those contracts:**
  - Payments may be taken only "as a progress payment after completion of the stage of work to which the progress payment relates" (r 41HE(2)).
  - **Any payment, including a deposit, is barred until RBI or fidelity cover is in force and the document has been given** (Act s 54AC(2)).
- **Re-roofing (non-prescribed work):** **no statutory deposit cap was found.**
- **URLs:** [NT BR]; [NT BA]

### 6. Cooling-off — VERIFIED (none)
- **Value:** no statutory cooling-off for building contracts. The Building Act, Building Regulations, RBI Regs and Dispute Regs were searched for "cooling" and "withdraw" with no relevant hit.
- **What still applies:** only the ACL unsolicited consumer agreement 10-business-day termination right, which applies in all jurisdictions. The ACL itself was not re-verified here.
- **URLs:** [NT BA]; [NT BR]

### 7. Home warranty / residential building insurance — VERIFIED / CORRECTED
- **Scheme name:**
  - **Value:** **"authorised RBI policy"** (residential building insurance from an approved insurer) **or "fidelity certificate"** under an approved fidelity fund scheme (Act s 54AC, Pt 5A Divs 3–4).
  - **Correction:** round 1 described only the Fidelity Fund certificate. Both options exist in the Act.
- **Threshold — CORRECTED:**
  - **Value:** "Residential building work is building work, of at least **$25 000** in value, in connection with the construction of a residential building" (RBI r 5(2)).
  - **Old value:** $12,000. [NT AMD26] r 4: "Regulation 5(2) omit $12 000 insert $25 000".
  - Commenced 30 Mar 2026. The old regulation still applies to contracts entered into before that date (RBI r 95).
  - The NT Gov page was not read (blocked). The legislation confirms the change.
- **Covered buildings:** Class 1a or prescribed Class 2 buildings, attached Class 10 built at the same time, and supporting retaining walls (r 5(3)). Extensions are included (r 5(4)).
- **When the cover must be in place:**
  - The builder "must not carry out prescribed residential building work" unless a policy or certificate is in force and the RBI policy document or fidelity certificate copy has been given to the other party (s 54AC(1)).
  - The builder must not "demand or receive payment (whether as a deposit or otherwise)" until both are done (s 54AC(2); 85 penalty units).
- **Roofing-only covered? NO.** "Renovations or alterations to an existing relevant building that do not increase the floor area" are excluded, with the example "The re-cladding of roofs or walls" (RBI r 5(5)(b)).
- **Minimum cover:** $200,000 in total (RBI regs, cover provisions).
- **URLs:** [NT RBI]; [NT AMD26]; [NT BA]

### 8. Required documents, progress payments, variations — VERIFIED (prescribed work only)
- **Prescribed residential work** (new house of $25,000 or more):
  - RBI policy document or fidelity certificate copy, before work or any payment (s 54AC).
  - Contract with r 41H contents.
  - Standard progress payments (r 41HA): base 10%, frame 20%, enclosed 25% ("roof covering is fixed"), fixing 30%, practical completion 7%, then the balance. The parties may vary these with a signed progress payment agreement in the approved form, keeping at least 3% for the final stage (r 41HB).

- **Re-roofing:** no NT statutory document pack, progress payment or variation-in-writing rule was found. No NT consumer building guide is prescribed.
- **URLs:** [NT BR]; [NT BA]

### 9. Statutory warranties — VERIFIED (prescribed work only)
- **Value:** Act s 54B "consumer guarantees" apply to "all prescribed residential building work carried out by a residential builder" (work covered by RBI or fidelity cover). They are:
  - proper and workmanlike work, following plans and specifications
  - materials good, suitable and new
  - compliance with laws
  - reasonable care and skill
  - completion by the contract date
- **Effective periods (DRR r 7):**
  - non-completion: 90 days from when the builder ceases work;
  - defects: the construction period plus a defect period of "**1 year**" (non-structural) or "**6 years**" (structural);
  - extended by 30 days if the owner becomes aware of the defect within the last 30 days of the period.
- **Roof defects are structural.** "structural element" includes "a component (including weatherproofing) forming part of the external walls or roof of the building" (RBI r 6).
- **Re-roofing:** since re-roofing is not prescribed work, s 54B does not apply. Only the ACL consumer guarantees apply.
- **Mediation or conciliation:** may still be sought for any work requiring a building permit, of any value (DRR r 5).
- **URLs:** [NT BA]; [NT DRR]; [NT RBI]

In practice the practitioner is a certifying plumber.

### 11. Building permit for re-roofing — VERIFIED (statute); practice UNVERIFIED

### 12. Security of payment — VERIFIED
- **Act:** Construction Contracts (Security of Payments) Act 2004 (NT).
- **Owner-occupiers:** it applies to construction contracts "irrespective of whether it is written or oral" (s 9). There is **no residential or owner-occupier exclusion** in the Act or the 2005 Regulations (both searched for "residential", "dwelling" and "owner occupier"). The only exclusions are employees and contracts prescribed by regulation, and none are prescribed. **So it applies to homeowner jobs: yes (no exclusion found).**
- **Model:** a payment-dispute model, not claim-and-schedule. Where the contract is silent, Sch 1 Div 5 implied terms apply:
  - "within **10 working days** after receiving the payment claim" give a notice of dispute (and pay any undisputed part);
  - or "within **20 working days** after receiving the payment claim, pay the whole of the amount".
- **Payment terms:** terms over 30 working days are read down to 30 (s 13). Pay-if-paid and pay-when-paid clauses have no effect (s 12).
- **Adjudication:** apply "within **65 working days** after the dispute arises" (s 28).
- **No contracting out** (s 10).
- **URLs:** [NT SOP]; https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11704

## Changes vs round-1 notes (QLD and NT)

**QLD**
- **Deposit:** the round-1 "conflict" is resolved. The statute (Sch 1B s 33) sets 10% for level 1 (over $3,300 and under $20,000), 5% for level 2 ($20,000 or more), and 20% where off-site work is over 50%, for either level. The QHWS premium counts toward the cap. The 20% figure for jobs of $3,300 or less is QBCC guidance only.
- **Statutory warranty:** confirmed as 6 years structural / 1 year other, plus 6 months if the breach becomes apparent in the last 6 months (s 29). The round-1 "6 years 6 months" figure is the **QHWS cover period**, not the statutory warranty.
- **Consumer Building Guide:** **new version FSG0116_v2_10/26 "effective October 2026"**, with a signed home owner acknowledgement. Round 1 said v3 effective July 2023. It must be given **before the owner signs** a level 2 contract.
- **Insurance:**
  - Roofing-only work over $3,300 **is** QHWS primary insurable work. This covers re-roofs, restorations including painting, gutters, gutter-guard, skylights and fascias. Round 1 had this unverified.
  - The premium is due before the earlier of 10 business days after contract or the start of work.
  - Insurable value assumes the contractor supplies all materials.
- **Licensing:**
  - The $3,300 threshold is confirmed (QBCC Reg sch 1 s 2). Roofing classes are not among the any-value exceptions.
  - **Roof painting is excluded from both roofing licence scopes.** It needs a Painting and decorating licence.
- **Form 4:** **stormwater, gutters and downpipes are not "plumbing" or "drainage" under the P&D Act 2018.** Form 4 applies only to real plumbing work, e.g. a tank connected to the house supply. The deadline is confirmed: 10 business days, plus a copy to the owner within 20 business days.
- **BIF Act:** the s 61(2)(b) resident-owner exclusion covers **all of Chapter 3**, including payment claims and schedules, not just adjudication. Payment schedule: 15 business days. Default due date: 10 business days.
- **Contracts:**
  - The 2025 amendment changed "written form" to "in writing" (electronic contracts are fine).
  - A non-compliant contract has no effect.
  - Owners can waive cooling-off for **repair contracts** (s 39).
  - The contractor keeps out-of-pocket expenses + $100 on withdrawal.
- **WHS:**
  - QLD-specific falls rule (s 306D): 3m for "housing construction work", 2m for other work, any height on roofs over 26°.
  - Re-roofing an existing house is probably **not** "housing construction work", so the 2m threshold applies.
- **Permits:** non-structural re-roofing is accepted development under Building Reg 2021 sch 1 ss 7–8 (no approval needed), unless it is structural, changes height, or involves solar/PV.

**NT**
- **Thresholds:** **$12,000 became $25,000 from 30 March 2026.** This covers RBI/fidelity cover (RBI r 5), contractor registration (BR r 41E), written contracts (r 41J) and owner-builders (r 41P). Confirmed from the amending regs SL No. 2 of 2026. Transitional rules keep $12,000 for earlier contracts or work.
- **Roofing outside the regime:** **re-roofing an existing house is expressly outside** registration, written contract, deposit cap, RBI/fidelity cover and Part 5A consumer guarantees. The regulations' own example is "re-cladding of the roof". Round 1 inferred this only loosely, from permit triggers.
- **Insurance options:** cover can be an **RBI policy or a fidelity certificate**, not only a Fidelity Fund certificate. No payment of any kind is allowed before cover is in place and the document has been given.
- **Deposit:** the 5% cap **is** in the regulations (BR r 41HE). Round 1 had not found a government source. It applies only to new-house contracts. Progress-payment invoices need a declaration that the stage is complete.
- **Warranties:** consumer guarantee periods are 1 year non-structural and 6 years structural, after the construction period. Roof weatherproofing counts as structural. Non-completion: 90 days.
- **Security of payment:** the NT SOP Act has **no** owner-occupier exclusion. Implied terms (contract silent): notice of dispute within 10 working days, or full payment within 20 working days. Terms are capped at 30 working days. Adjudication: within 65 working days.
- **WHS: new from 17 July 2026.** The high risk construction work (SWMS) fall threshold is **3m for residential construction work** (2m otherwise).
- **Permits:** a permit is required for all building work including repairs in building control areas (s 55). There is no re-roofing exemption in the Regs.
