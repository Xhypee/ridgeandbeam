# Australian Storm/Hail/Insurance Roofing: Claims, Getting Paid, Tax/Invoicing and Warranties (by state)

Research date: 2026-11-06. Method note: page fetches to insurancecouncil.com.au, rba.gov.au and viabuild.au were blocked by this session's network policy, so findings below rest on search-result extracts of those pages (including primary ICA/RBA/ASIC/ATO/ACCC pages surfaced in search) plus law-firm and industry guides. Items not confirmed against a source are kept in Gaps or flagged "UNVERIFIED". Several items are recent reforms (flagged "REFORM").

## 1. How Australian home insurance roof-damage claims work (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
The AU model has no ACV/RCV depreciation split, no mortgagee-endorsed cheques and no public-adjuster industry. A claim runs: homeowner lodges claim with insurer, insurer appoints an assessor/loss adjuster and often an insurer "panel" builder, insurer issues a scope of works, then EITHER the insurer manages repairs through its panel builder (the insurer pays the builder directly and the homeowner pays only the excess) OR the insurer cash-settles to the homeowner, who then engages and pays their own roofer. Disputes (storm damage vs wear and tear, inadequate cash settlement, poor make-safe) escalate to the insurer's internal dispute resolution (IDR), then to AFCA. The governing industry code is the General Insurance Code of Practice (the 2020 Code, current version Feb 2025). A redrafted, enforceable Code was lodged with ASIC in Oct 2026 and is not expected to commence before about 2028.

### Cited Findings
**Code of Practice status and timeframes**
- Current Code: the ICA-published "General Insurance Code of Practice February 2025" (the 2020 Code as amended) — [Steadfast copy of Feb 2025 Code (PDF)](https://steadfastclaims.com.au/media/b3flbsjj/general-insurance-code-of-practice-february-2025.pdf); [ICA Code page](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/cop/)
- Claim decision is due within 4 months of receiving the claim, with a 12-month outer limit in specific circumstances. If the insurer cannot decide in time it must tell the customer in writing about the complaints process. Where timeframes are impractical (complex claims) the insurer agrees a reasonable alternative timetable with the customer — [ICA Code page](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/cop/); [Berrill & Watson](https://www.berrillwatson.com.au/supertalk-blog/2025/april/insurer-response-time-limits/); [Fortis Risk Solutions](https://fortisrisksolutions.com.au/how-long-can-an-insurer-take-to-decide-your-claim/)
- Once all information is received and enquiries are complete, the decision is due within 10 business days — [Berrill & Watson](https://www.berrillwatson.com.au/supertalk-blog/2025/april/insurer-response-time-limits/)
- Progress updates at least every 20 business days. This figure appears in an older insurer summary, so treat it as indicative for the current Code — [HBA Legal summary (older)](https://www.hbalegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/What-does-the-Insurance-Code-of-Practice-mean-for-Insurers.pdf)
- Catastrophes are declared by the ICA (fire, flood, earthquake, cyclone, severe storm and hail) when an event causes a large number of claims across multiple insurers. An "Extraordinary Catastrophe" declaration extends claim decision timeframes from 4 to 12 months. Property claims finalised within one month of a catastrophe carry a review entitlement — [ICA, "New benchmark for catastrophe preparedness" (Oct 2023)](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/The-new-benchmark-for-catastrophe-preparedness-in-Australia_Oct-2023.pdf); [ICA submission to 2022 floods inquiry](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICA-submission-Inquiry-into-insurers-responses-to-2022-major-floods-claims.pdf)
- REFORM: the ICA submitted its redrafted, "strengthened" Code to ASIC for approval on 22 Oct 2026 — [ICA Code submissions update](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/code-of-practice/code-submissions/); [Insurance Business – lodgement delayed](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/ica-delays-code-lodgement-as-industryconsumer-divide-deepens-584518.aspx)
- REFORM: a 24-month transition is anticipated, with commencement expected around 2028 — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com.au/insurance/redrafted-general-insurance-code-of-practice-for-insurers.html); [Insurance Business – "industry has until 2028"](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/australias-insurance-rulebook-is-getting-teeth--and-the-industry-has-until-2028-to-get-ready-577740.aspx)
- REFORM: the redraft makes the Code enforceable as part of consumer insurance contracts (except the Principles and Section 10). Home and motor claims would be deemed accepted after 12 months without a decision (subject to exceptions). The 4-month benchmark stays. The draft says insurers are not liable for timeframe breaches while an Extraordinary Event declaration is in place — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com.au/insurance/redrafted-general-insurance-code-of-practice-for-insurers.html); [Proposed Code (PDF, Oct 2026)](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Proposed-General-Insurance-Code-of-Practice.pdf); [Curium – GICOP 2026 changes](https://getcurium.com/news/general-insurance-code-of-practice-changes-2026/)
- Submitters criticised the draft because the "Claims fulfilment" paragraphs (payment or repair) set no timeframes — [Individual submission on draft Code (PDF)](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Individual-2_Non-confidential_Submission_Redacted.pdf)

**Cash settlement vs insurer-managed repair (panel builders)**
- AFCA says cash settlement should "not be a first option". A policy discretion to repair, replace or pay "does not mean an insurer can unilaterally decide how to settle the claim". When an insurer cash-settles it gives no guarantee for the works and the risk passes to the policyholder, so AFCA tests the insurer's reasons against utmost good faith — [AFCA Approach to general insurance claims handling, July 2025 (PDF)](https://www.afca.org.au/media/2140/download)
- In one determination, AFCA increased an inadequate cash settlement by an additional 10% on the quoted figure so it would cover the reasonable cost of repairs — [AFCA determination (PDF)](https://www.afca.org.au/media/464/download). A broker page claims uplifts of 10-20% and up to $5,000 in expert costs. That range is UNVERIFIED against AFCA material — [CIB](https://cib.com.au/learn/roof-condition-insurance-claims/)
- ASIC REP 768 "Navigating the storm" (Aug 2023) analysed more than 218,000 home claims (Jan-Mar 2022) from six insurers covering 63% of the market. It found weaknesses in communication, oversight (including of third parties), complaint recognition, treatment of vulnerable customers and resourcing — [ASIC REP 768 (PDF)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tgrozota/rep768-published-16-august-2023.pdf); [APO listing](https://apo.org.au/node/324058)
- REFORM / 2026: in ASIC's review of Cyclone Jasper claims, 63% of home claims were fully or partially cash-settled. ASIC warned that cash settlements must not short-change homeowners, that many cash-settlement fact sheets give minimal information on the right to have a cash settlement reviewed, and that oversight of builders and repairers has improved but gaps remain in oversight of independent experts — [ASIC 26-202MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-202mr-asic-warns-insurers-cash-settlements-should-not-short-change-homeowners-in-need); [ASIC "Beyond the payout"](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/beyond-the-payout-asic-warns-home-insurers-to-reduce-cash-settlement-risks); [ASIC follow-up article](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/home-insurance-claims-handling-improvements-need-to-go-further-asic-says)

**Make-safe and wear-and-tear disputes**
- AFCA case studies hold insurers responsible for their builders' make-safe work. In one, an inadequate make-safe on a stormwater ingress claim caused more damage. In another, the insurer's builder failed to remove asbestos safely and contaminated the home, and AFCA found cash settlement unfair in those circumstances — [AFCA Approach July 2025](https://www.afca.org.au/media/2140/download); [insuranceNEWS on AFCA guidelines](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/local/afca-issues-claims-guidelines-case-studies)
- Where the insurer relies only on a wear-and-tear exclusion and its evidence is not persuasive, AFCA will likely require the claim to be paid — [AFCA Approach July 2025](https://www.afca.org.au/media/2140/download)
- Make-safe (for example tarping) protects both the building and the claim. Stopping further water entry is part of the policyholder's duty of reasonable care — [CIB](https://cib.com.au/learn/roof-condition-insurance-claims/)

### Inferences
- Data model change: replace "deductible" with "excess" (a fixed amount the policyholder pays, usually to the repairer or deducted from a cash settlement). Remove ACV/RCV and recoverable depreciation. Add settlement type = {insurer-managed repair (panel builder), homeowner's own builder with insurer approval, cash settlement}, plus a cash-settlement-review flag, a catastrophe-declaration flag (an ICA CAT code), and Code timeframe clocks (4-month decision, 10 business days after information complete, 20-business-day updates).
- Payment routing: with an insurer-managed repair, the roofer is a subcontractor to the insurer's builder or repair network and is paid by that builder or network on its terms, while the homeowner pays the excess. With a cash settlement, the roofer contracts with and is paid by the homeowner. That contract is "residential building work" under state home building law, which brings in licensing, deposits, home warranty insurance and statutory warranties. Nothing resembles mortgage-company cheque endorsement.
- Roof claims are especially exposed to wear-and-tear and maintenance exclusions. A roofer's evidence pack (dated photos, storm or BOM event data, an expert report) matters because AFCA weighs the persuasiveness of the insurer's evidence.

### Gaps
- No sourced confirmation of a Code timeframe for make-safe, or of the IDR response deadline. From prior knowledge (UNVERIFIED here), ASIC RG 271 sets a 30-calendar-day IDR response time, after which the complainant can go to AFCA.
- I could not confirm the current Code's cash-settlement-specific obligations (for example a fact-sheet requirement and a review window). ASIC refers to a "right to have a cash settlement reviewed", but the exact period was not retrieved.
- New-for-old vs indemnity policy wording, and how excess is collected in practice, were not covered by retrieved sources. Most AU home policies are "replacement" or new-for-old with a sum insured, but that is UNVERIFIED here.
- No source retrieved on assignment of rights or benefits to roofers. Assignment of benefits does not appear to be an established AU practice like it is in the US (UNVERIFIED).

## 2. Rules on contractors and claims: licensing, acting for homeowners, storm-chasers, unsolicited sales

### Takeaway
Since 1 Jan 2022 "claims handling and settling" is a financial service requiring an AFS licence. Builders and repairers who only give expert opinions or quotes are exempt unless they can reject claims, but anyone acting as a paid claims intermediary for a homeowner needs licensing. Door-knocking after storms is governed by the ACL's unsolicited consumer agreement (UCA) rules: 10-business-day cooling-off, no payment and no supply during that period, and restricted calling hours. State Fair Trading agencies routinely warn about post-storm roofing scams.

### Cited Findings
- From 1 Jan 2022, under the Financial Sector Reform (Hayne Royal Commission Response) Act 2020, claims handling and settling is a financial service needing an AFS licence — [ASIC INFO 253 (June 2025 version, PDF)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/q2ybygwo/info253-published-16-june-2025.pdf); [Treasury/ASIC paper on Rec 4.8 (PDF)](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-10/t406602_asic.pdf)
- Experts who give opinions to help an insurer assess a claim (engineers, builders, repairers) are exempt unless authorised to reject claims — [Flex Insurance](https://flexinsurance.com.au/news/claims-handling-and-settling); [AFSL House](https://afslhouse.com.au/do-you-need-an-afsl-for-claims-handling-and-settling-understanding-the-requirements/)
- Claims managers and intermediaries need a claims-handling authorisation. Qualifying brokers are exempt — [AFSL House](https://afslhouse.com.au/do-you-need-an-afsl-for-claims-handling-and-settling-understanding-the-requirements/). The exact exemption wording should be checked in INFO 253 (I could not retrieve the full text).
- UCA cooling-off is 10 business days. The supplier cannot request or accept payment during it. It extends to 3 months if the consumer was not told of the cooling-off right, and to 6 months in some other cases (for example services supplied, or goods over $500, inside the cooling-off period) — [ACCC door-to-door guide (PDF)](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Door%20to%20door%20sales%20-%20a%20guide%20for%20consumers%20B5%20booklet.pdf); [ACCC media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/misleading-and-high-pressure-unsolicited-sales-practices-widespread); [ACCC business page](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/telemarketing-and-door-to-door-sales)
- A UCA requires a total price over $100, or one that cannot be determined when the agreement is made. Default permitted hours are Mon-Fri 9am-6pm and Sat 9am-5pm, with no calls on Sundays or public holidays. States can vary the hours by regulation (ACL s 73). Sources disagree on whether the hours apply to deals under $100: Queensland says they do, Consumer Action says they do not — [QLD Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/your-rights/consumer-rights-complaints-and-scams/buying-products-and-services/understanding-sales-practices/door-to-door-telemarketing-sales-rules); [Consumer Action "Do Not Knock"](https://donotknock.consumeraction.org.au/2012/09/18/loophole-in-australian-consumer-law-allows-door-knockers-to-visit-outside-allowed-hours/); [NSW Gov UCA page](https://www.nsw.gov.au/legal-and-justice/consumer-rights-and-protection/guarantees-contracts-and-warranties/unsolicited-consumer-agreements); [WA Consumer Protection](https://consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/publications/unsolicited-selling-formerly-known-door-door-sales)
- Queensland notes that a contractual cooling-off period does not apply to emergency repairs where a government has declared a state of emergency. This came from QLD guidance and could not be matched to an ACL provision — [QLD Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/your-rights/consumer-rights-complaints-and-scams/buying-products-and-services/understanding-sales-practices/door-to-door-telemarketing-sales-rules)
- REFORM: in June 2025 the ACCC began a review of unsolicited selling and lead generation. It says businesses cannot avoid UCA obligations by using third-party lead generators. Consumer Action Law Centre lodged a designated complaint in March 2025 — [ACCC consultation paper June 2025 (PDF)](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/public-registers/documents/ACCC%20consultation%20paper%20-%20Unsolicited%20selling%20and%20lead%20generation%20-%20June%202025.pdf?amp=&download=y); [CALC designated complaint (PDF)](https://consumeraction.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/20250317-CALC-ACCC-Designated-Complaint-Unsolicited-sales-public-version.pdf); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/burying-the-lead-generation)
- NSW Fair Trading requires a licence for residential work over $5,000 and for specialist trades regardless of cost. It warns that unlicensed traders target storm- and flood-affected communities offering roof repairs, and advises checking licences on Verify.licence, avoiding people who turn up uninvited, never paying before meeting the tradesperson, and checking with the insurer before authorising repairs — [NSW Fair Trading – flood damage scams](https://www.getregional.com.au/nsw-fair-trading-warns-consumers-about-flood-damage-scams/); [NSW Fair Trading "Beware charming conmen"](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/fair-trading/news/beware-charming-conmen-offering-deals-too-good-to-be-true)
- The ICA has reported fake tradespeople door-knocking after hailstorms, offering roof inspections and repairs — [Insurance Business – NSW hailstorm scams](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/nsw-hailstorm-victims-urged-to-beware-of-scammers-122759.aspx)

### Inferences
- Canvassing workflow: any door-knock or cold-call lead that results in an agreement over $100 should be treated as a UCA. The CRM should enforce calling hours, generate the prescribed UCA disclosure and cancellation form, block deposits and job scheduling until 10 business days have passed (except where an emergency-repair exception is confirmed), and log the consumer's own invitation where the contact was solicited.
- A roofer should not hold itself out as negotiating or settling claims for the homeowner (a "claims advocate" or public-adjuster role) without AFS licensing. Supplying a quote or scope, or an expert damage report, is the safe position.

### Gaps
- QLD (QBCC) licensing thresholds for roofing work were not verified in this pass. The user-cited $3,300 figure is UNVERIFIED, and per-state licence thresholds are likely covered by an adjacent researcher.
- Storm-chaser warnings from other states (VIC, QLD, SA, WA) were not retrieved.
- The ACL/regulation text for an urgent-repairs exception to UCA rules was not confirmed.

## 3. Security of payment (SOP), by state, and the absence of mechanic's liens

### Takeaway
Every state and territory has an SOP Act giving a statutory right to progress payments, short payment-schedule deadlines and fast adjudication, with an adjudication certificate enforceable as a judgment debt. Coverage of homeowner work differs. NSW removed its owner-occupier exemption in 2021, so it now applies. Queensland (BIF Act s 61) and Victoria exclude resident-owner or domestic contracts at head-contract level. Tasmania covers owner-occupiers with a longer schedule period. The NT uses a different payment-dispute model. A roofer working as a subcontractor to an insurer's builder generally has SOP rights against that builder in every state. No source confirmed a US-style mechanic's lien. Treat Australia as having none and the SOP regime as the closest equivalent.

### Inferences
- Replace the "mechanic's lien" module with an SOP module: state-specific payment-claim templates (in NSW, QLD and others the claim must identify the work and the amount and, where required, state it is made under the Act), a reference-date or claim-cycle calendar, payment-schedule deadline countdowns per state (10 or 15 business days, longer for Tas owner-occupiers), and adjudication-application deadlines. Use business-day calendars with state public holidays.
- Gate SOP features by counterparty type. Against a builder, insurer repair network or commercial owner, SOP applies everywhere. Against a resident homeowner it applies in NSW and Tas, does not apply in QLD (s 61) or VIC (domestic), and must be checked in WA, SA and ACT.
- Without liens, unpaid homeowner debts fall back on contract and debt recovery (state tribunals such as NCAT or VCAT, or courts). A caveat is only possible where a contract contains a charge clause over the land. This is general knowledge and UNVERIFIED.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly confirmed "Australia has no mechanic's lien". The search found nothing on liens, caveats or charge clauses, so it remains an inference.
- Homeowner coverage under the WA 2021 Act, SA 2009 and ACT 2009 is unconfirmed.
- NSW and VIC SOP reform status for 2024-2026 (for example any NSW amendment bill implementing earlier reviews) was not retrieved.
- Payment-claim content requirements per state, and statutory due dates for NSW and VIC, were not retrieved from sources. Prior knowledge (UNVERIFIED): NSW head contractor 15 business days and subcontractor 20 business days.

## 4. Tax, invoicing, surcharging, payment methods and consumer finance

### Takeaway
GST-registered roofers must issue compliant tax invoices showing their ABN, adding the buyer's identity or ABN at $1,000 or more. Builders paying subcontractors lodge a TPAR by 28 August. REFORM: card surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa is banned from 1 October 2026 under the RBA's March 2026 conclusions. Until then surcharges were capped at the cost of acceptance. BNPL became regulated credit on 10 June 2025, so roofer finance partners (Brighte, humm, Zip, Plenti) now operate under credit licences.

### Cited Findings
- Tax invoices: if asked, one must be issued within 28 days, unless the sale is $82.50 (GST-inclusive) or less. For sales under $1,000 the required items are: words showing it is intended to be a tax invoice, seller identity, seller ABN, issue date, a description including quantity and price, the GST amount (or a statement that the price includes GST), and the extent to which each sale is taxable. For $1,000 or more, add the buyer's identity or ABN — [ATO Tax invoices](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/tax-invoices); [Xero guide](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/tax-invoice-requirements/)
- TPAR: businesses primarily in building and construction that pay contractors for building and construction services must lodge by 28 August each year. Employee wages are not included. Where an invoice covers labour and materials, the total is reported. Nil reports may be required. Amounts withheld for no-ABN suppliers are included — [ATO TPAR building and construction](https://www.ato.gov.au/Business/Reports-and-returns/Taxable-payments-annual-report/Work-out-if-you-need-to-lodge-a-TPAR/Building-and-construction-services/); [ATO payments to report](https://www.ato.gov.au/Business/Reports-and-returns/Taxable-payments-annual-report/Work-out-if-you-need-to-lodge-a-TPAR/Payments-businesses-need-to-report-in-their-TPAR/); [MBA NSW](https://www.mbansw.asn.au/media/industry-news/taxable-payments-annual-report-due)
- REFORM: from 1 Oct 2026 card surcharges are removed for eftpos, Mastercard and Visa. Weekend and public-holiday surcharges and booking or service fees are not covered by the ban. Foreign-card interchange changes start 1 April 2027 — [RBA FAQs, March 2026 conclusions](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/faqs/); [RBA conclusions – surcharging chapter](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/surcharging.html); [NAB](https://www.nab.com.au/business/payments-and-merchants/merchant-support-centre/rba-surcharging-changes); [Tyro](https://www.tyro.com/blog/rba-surcharge-ban-confirmed-what-your-business-needs-to-know/)
- Amex, UnionPay and PayPal: the RBA FAQ says these schemes also decided to remove surcharging, and NAB says the removal covers Amex and UnionPay. Merchantrates says Amex and PayPal are excluded (conflict) — [RBA FAQ](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/faqs/); [Merchantrates](https://merchantrates.com.au/rba-2026/surcharge-ban-explained/)
- Mechanism: the RBA removed its prohibition on no-surcharge rules, so enforcement runs through card-scheme rules and merchant agreements. Before 1 Oct 2026, surcharges were allowed but capped at the merchant's cost of acceptance — [Square AU](https://squareup.com/au/en/the-bottom-line/managing-your-finances/rba-card-surcharging); [MST](https://www.mst.com.au/blog/rba-card-surcharge-ban-australia/)
- BNPL: the Treasury Laws Amendment (Responsible Buy Now Pay Later and Other Measures) Act 2024 applies from 10 June 2025. Providers need an Australian credit licence and AFCA membership, with transitional relief for applications lodged before that date. The 62-day short-term and account-charge exemptions do not apply to BNPL — [Dentons](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2025/march/13/buy-now-pay-later--a-snapshot-into-the-reforms); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/buy-now-pay-later-reform-what-you-need-to-know/); [Viridian Lawyers](https://viridianlawyers.com/blog/bnpl-reforms-low-cost-credit-contracts-australian-startups/)
- humm retired its old BNPL product and launched a new one on 10 June 2025. Merchants had to replace point-of-sale material and use a new merchant portal. humm group holds multiple credit licences. Brighte, Hummgroup, Plenti and Zip are listed as BNPL code-compliant members — [humm merchant FAQs](https://www.shophumm.com/au/merchant-support-faqs/); [Dwyer Harris](https://www.dwyerharris.com/blog/regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later)

### Inferences
- Invoicing module: default all consumer prices to GST-inclusive, record the ABN, switch the invoice template at the $1,000 threshold (always including buyer details is simplest), and record a contractor ABN for each subcontractor payment to feed a TPAR export.
- Payments: from 1 Oct 2026, disable automatic card-surcharge line items for eftpos, Visa and Mastercard and absorb or price in card costs. Prefer bank transfer (BSB/account), PayID or PayTo to reduce card cost.
- Finance: the roofer acts as a merchant or introducer for a licensed lender. Avoid giving credit assistance or advice beyond the provider's point-of-sale arrangements. Exact exemption scope is a Gap.

## 5. Warranties: statutory (by state), manufacturer, and defect liability

### Takeaway
Statutory warranties for residential building work are implied by state law and periods differ. NSW: 6 years for major defects, 2 years for others. VIC: 10 years. QLD: 6 years structural, 1 year non-structural. SA: 5 years. ACT: 6 years structural, 2 years non-structural. WA and Tas: about 6 years. Manufacturer warranties (for example BlueScope COLORBOND) are separate, depend on location and coastal exposure, and require owner registration (within 12 months of installation per BlueScope). Installer workmanship warranties are separate again.

### Cited Findings
- NSW: Home Building Act s 18E sets 6 years for major defects and 2 years for other defects — [RCM Lawyers Home Building Act guide](https://www.rcm.com.au/guides/home-building-act-guide/); [Boettcher Law](https://boettcher.law/building-and-construction-law/building-defects/)
- VIC: a single 10-year limitation from the occupancy permit or certificate of final inspection (Building Act 1993), with no major/minor split — [Boettcher Law](https://boettcher.law/building-and-construction-law/building-defects/); [Built Simple 2026 guide](https://builtsimple.com.au/blog/construction-defect-liability-australia-2026/)
- QLD: 6 years for structural defects and 1 year otherwise. Discovering a concealed defect does not generally restart the period — [Stonegate Legal](https://stonegatelegal.com.au/statutory-warranties-for-residential-building-work-in-queensland/). The "6 years 6 months" figure in the brief was NOT confirmed. It may reflect QHWS cover or a 6-month extension for defects appearing late in the period, which is UNVERIFIED.
- SA: 5 years from completion under the Building Work Contractors Act 1995 — [Built Simple](https://builtsimple.com.au/blog/construction-defect-liability-australia-2026/); [Yorcon](https://www.yorcon.com.au/blog/defects-liability-period-australia)
- WA and TAS: 6 years (single tier). This comes from a builder guide and is UNVERIFIED against primary law — [Built Simple](https://builtsimple.com.au/blog/construction-defect-liability-australia-2026/)
- ACT: 6 years structural, 2 years non-structural from completion — [LC Lawyers (ACT)](https://lclawyers.com.au/variable-limitation-period-domestic-statutory-warranties-australian-capital-territory/)
- COLORBOND: register within 12 months of installation. If the installer supplied a pre-approved warranty, the owner activates it with a code. Warranty periods depend on end use and location — [Colorbond FAQ](https://colorbond.com/faq). One consumer guide cites up to 45 years for some products (UNVERIFIED) — [Vivify Roofing](https://www.vivifyroofing.com.au/colorbond/colorbond-ultra-roof-material/). Another installer site says 18 months for registration, which conflicts with BlueScope — [Concept Kits](https://conceptkits.com.au/colorbond-warranty/)
- BlueScope coastal guidance: standard COLORBOND for roofs more than 200 m from breaking surf, COLORBOND Ultra for 100-200 m, and stainless or other options closer than that, measured from the high-water mark — [SteelSelect (BlueScope)](https://steelselect.com.au/articles/specifying-marine-industrial-environments-bluescope)
- Installer workmanship warranties are separate from material warranties and vary by installer — [Cloud9 Roofing](https://www.cloud9roofing.com.au/post/looking-for-colorbond-roofing-here-are-7-things-you-should-know-about-coastal-performance)

### Inferences
- The warranty module needs four layers per job: (1) the state statutory warranty, with the period by state and defect class; (2) home warranty insurance where triggered (state thresholds, Gap); (3) manufacturer material warranty, with registration task, reminder at 12 months for COLORBOND, and coastal distance captured on site; (4) the contractual workmanship warranty and defects liability period.
