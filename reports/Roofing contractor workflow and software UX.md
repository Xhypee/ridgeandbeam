# Win roofers by closing the handoff gaps

US roofing jobs are a relay. A lead becomes an inspection, then a measurement, a three-tier proposal, a state-regulated contract, often an insurance claim, a material order, a weather-dependent crew slot, a photo record, a change order, several checks, a warranty registration, a commission and a review. The software market has split into a few incumbents that do most of this, AccuLynx and JobNimbus, and a ring of point tools around them: measurement, photos, canvassing, proposals, Xactimate and QuickBooks. Users mostly hate the same few things. They are surprised by price hikes and locked in by contracts. Mobile apps lose or stall photos on weak signal. Data has to be typed again between tools. And nobody can see when the insurance money will arrive. The research points Ridge & Beam to one strategy: **become the single source of truth from one measurement to one paid commission, priced flat and month-to-month, with an offline-first field app and a homeowner tracker that explains the claim.** Ridge & Beam already has the skeleton: a CRM, a pipeline, a schedule, a calculator-driven estimate, public acceptance links and invoices. The most valuable near-term work changes data models more than it adds screens. Move the insurance claim from the customer to the job and give it its own stages and money ledger. Turn single estimates into good-better-best tiers with financing shown. Make contracts and deposits state-aware. Replace manual payment tracking with milestone and insurance-check reconciliation. **A caveat runs through the whole report.** The research team could not fetch most vendor, review-site and Reddit pages, so many figures come from search snippets, vendor marketing or affiliate review sites. Statute text, lender guides, manufacturer warranty PDFs and vendor help docs are the solid ground, and the recommendations below are graded to match.

## A roof replacement passes through about fifteen handoffs and four roles

The end-to-end flow has two halves. The front half runs from lead to signed contract and belongs to the sales rep. The back half runs from contract to paid, warrantied and reviewed, and belongs to the office admin or production manager, the crew lead and the owner. The table below condenses the sequence from the research, with the role that owns each step and the artifact it produces. The artifacts matter most: nearly every pain point in the evidence happens when an artifact has to be copied by hand from one step, or one tool, into the next.

| Stage | Primary owner | Key artifact | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead intake | Office / rep | Lead with source and cost | Slow response; manual re-entry from lead sites |
| Inspection | Rep | ~50 photos, test-square counts | Photo upload failures on the roof |
| Measurement | Rep / office | Aerial or photo report (area, pitch, edge LF) | Hours-to-days wait; re-keying PDF numbers |
| Estimate and proposal | Rep | Good-better-best proposal with financing | Margin errors; slow proposal delivery |
| Contract | Rep / homeowner | E-signed contract, contingency agreement | State deposit caps and cancellation rules |
| Insurance claim | Rep / office | Carrier scope, supplements, checks | Months of paperwork-gated cash delay |
| Material order | Production | Distributor order, delivery photos | Wrong quantities and colors, returns |
| Scheduling | Production | 2-week rolling install calendar | Weather cascades; homeowner not told |
| Install and documentation | Crew lead | Photo timeline, checklists | Language barriers; no offline mode |
| Change order | Crew lead / homeowner | Decking per-sheet approval | Disputes when not pre-priced |
| Invoicing and collection | Office | Milestone invoices, lien waivers | Manual tracking; QuickBooks sync gaps |
| Warranty | Office | Manufacturer registration | Missed registration windows |
| Commission and job cost | Owner / rep | Budget vs actual, rep statement | Late costs delay or dispute payouts |
| Review and referral | Office | Review request with photos | Asked at the wrong moment |

### Lead generation runs on cost per booked job and speed to lead

Roofing has the most expensive leads in home services. LocaliQ's analysis of 3,211 home-service Google Ads campaigns put the **average roofing cost per lead at $228.15** (relayed by [99calls](https://blog.99calls.com/2026/02/02/roofing-lead-generation-costs-in-2026/) and [ActiveProspect](https://activeprospect.com/blog/roofing-leads-cost/); the primary report was not read). Owners therefore reason in **cost per booked job, which is cost per lead divided by close rate**. On that measure an exclusive $250 lead closing at 25% beats a $90 shared lead closing at 5% ([ActiveProspect](https://activeprospect.com/blog/roofing-leads-cost/)). Speed to lead is the most-cited lever. One vendor study of 2,847 leads reports an **average roofer response time of 8.7 hours**, with the top tenth responding in under 15 minutes ([SalesCaptain](https://blog.salescaptain.com/?p=1731), methodology unverified). ServiceTitan says only about half of contractors respond within an hour ([ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/speed-to-lead-ai-virtual-agent)). Treat the exact numbers as directional. The direction itself is consistent across every source.

Homeowners shop around. Roofing Contractor's homeowner survey reports that **66% would gather three quotes** ([2026 survey](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/101774-homeowners-survey-the-roofing-customers-journey-in-2026), snippet only). Its 2025 edition found that **65% are more likely to call a roofer that shows pricing on its website** ([2025 survey](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/100649-2025-homeowner-roofing-survey-tracking-the-journey)). That edition was sponsored by Roofle, which sells instant quotes, so weigh it accordingly. Storm-restoration companies work differently. They draw territories over hail swaths and send canvassers with GPS-tracked apps such as SPOTIO and SalesRabbit, which push leads into the production CRM ([SPOTIO](https://spotio.com/solutions/storm-restoration-crm/); [SalesRabbit](https://salesrabbit.com/field-sales-platform)). Pay splits accordingly. One posting pays $100 per canvassed appointment that becomes an inspection, plus a bonus once the job signs and the carrier approves ([CareerBuilder](https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/roof-inspector-outside-sales-uncapped-commissions-tucson-az--cf1d4961-1264-4144-a2bd-77eb0f76055c)). Attribution of who knocked the door is therefore a payroll fact, not a marketing nicety.

### The inspection produces evidence for two audiences

On site, the rep photographs the whole roof. One contractor's practice is **about 50 photos per dwelling**, damaged and undamaged, plus a 10x10 ft test square on each slope with the hail hits marked ([Frogtown Roofing](https://frogtownroofing.com/?p=789)). Carriers commonly approve full replacement at about **8 or more hits per 100 sq ft**, but this varies by carrier and policy ([Disaster911](https://disaster911.net/blog/hail-damage-roof-claim-adjuster-checklist-michigan/)). A May 2026 Claims Journal piece warns that test squares are high-variance and that inspectors should cover as much roof as they can ([Claims Journal](https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/05/06/337211.htm)). The same photo set serves the homeowner at the kitchen table and the adjuster as evidence. A slope-tagged, annotated, shareable photo report is therefore one of the most reused artifacts in the business. The researchers found no sourced material on how reps actually use phones on steep roofs, such as gloves, glare or one-handed use. Those constraints are strongly implied by the work but are not proven by the evidence.

### Measurement is a per-job tax with a waiting period

Contractors either order aerial or photo reports or measure from satellite imagery themselves. Reported per-report prices span **about $9-13 for RoofSnap, $13-19 for Roofr, $18-20 for GAF QuickMeasure, roughly $18-87 for EagleView and $25-139 per Hover project** ([roofingsoftwareguide](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/roundups/roofing-software-pricing-comparison/); [theroofingbrief](https://theroofingbrief.com/2026-aerial-roof-measurement-software-report/)). These come from affiliate-leaning sites, and the figures conflict from one source to the next. Turnaround runs from under an hour (QuickMeasure) to 24-72 hours (standard EagleView) ([Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/hover-vs-eagleview/)). The report schema is consistent and well documented. An EagleView Premium report gives total area, facet count, pitch per facet, linear feet of ridge, hip, valley, rake, eave and step flashing, and a suggested-waste table. That table is **valid only for asphalt shingles on areas of 3/12 pitch or steeper** ([EagleView sample report](https://www.eagleview.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Premium-Sample-Report-Residential-1.pdf)). The schema is what matters for Ridge & Beam. If the built-in calculator emits the same fields, then estimates, orders and crew pay can consume either source interchangeably, and reps can quote at the table before a paid report arrives. No independent accuracy test of any measurement vendor was found. Tree canopy is the most-reported failure point for aerial reports ([theroofingbrief](https://theroofingbrief.com/eagleview-vs-hover-vs-roofsnap/)).

### Estimates and proposals: simple arithmetic, expensive mistakes, three tiers

The core math is settled. A square is 100 sq ft. Gross squares equal net squares times one plus waste. Bundles are gross squares times bundles per square (usually 3), **always rounded up because suppliers do not break bundles** ([Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/mi-roofing-contractor/planning-estimating/material-takeoffs-squares-waste-factors)). Waste runs from about 8% on a simple gable to 17% on complex roofs ([getclue](https://www.getclue.com/calculators/asphalt-shingle-calculator)). Steep and complex roofs add 20-30% to price, and installed asphalt is commonly cited at **$350-$1,200 per square** in 2026 ([JobNimbus](https://www.jobnimbus.com/blog/how-much-does-a-new-roof-really-cost-in-2025-the-numbers-you-need-to-know); [SubcontractorHub](https://www.subcontractorhub.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-replace-a-roof)). The mistakes the software can prevent follow from these formulas. They include applying shingle waste to low-slope sections, forgetting starter, ridge or drip edge, failing to round to whole bundles or rolls, and leaving out tear-off layers, steep charges and decking allowances.

For proposals, the NRCIA calls **good-better-best the professional standard** ([NRCIA](https://www.nrcia.org/?p=3656)). The tiers differ by shingle line, system components and manufacturer warranty, and the middle tier is positioned as the recommendation ([JobNimbus](https://www.jobnimbus.com/blog/good-better-best-roofing-estimate-example)). Claims that 68% of buyers pick the middle tier, or that premium warranties lift close rates 18-30%, are unverified vendor or blog figures ([RoofPredict](https://roofpredict-v2.pages.dev/blog/unlock-shingle-sales-good-better-best-framework-for-contractors); [Pipeline Marketing](https://pipeline-marketing.pages.dev/blog/roofing-warranty-types/)). Warranty tiers are gated by certification. GAF Golden Pledge, for example, requires a Master Elite contractor and a full GAF system ([GAF brochure](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/document-library/documents/brochures-&-literature/golden-pledge-limited-warranty-brochure-reswt161b.pdf)). The proposal builder should therefore offer only the warranty tiers the company is certified to sell.

### Contracts and financing are where the law shows up in the UI

Deposit and cancellation rules are state-specific, and this is the most reliable evidence in the whole corpus because it is statute text. **New York bars roofers from requiring any deposit** and gives insurance-paid contracts a three-business-day cancellation right ([NY GBL §771-b](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/771-B)). **California caps the down payment at the lesser of $1,000 or 10%** ([CA SB 601 text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB601/2023)). **Tennessee bars requiring any payment** until the insurer confirms coverage in writing or the three-day cancellation window has passed ([TN §62-6-601](https://www.tn.gov/lawsandpolicies/laws-a-c/laws-contractors-and-contracting/residential-roofing-services/62-6-601--part-definitions-111.html)). Ohio mandates specific cancellation language, including a right tied to insurance claims ([Ohio Legislature](https://www.legislature.ohio.gov/download?key=20484)). Colorado gives a 72-hour rescission right after the homeowner receives written notice of a claim denial ([C.R.S. 6-22-105](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2022/title-6/article-22/section-6-22-105/)). Texas requires a bold deductible notice on insurance contracts of $1,000 or more ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/roofing-and-insurance-know-the-law.html)). The federal FTC Cooling-Off Rule was not verified in this research. Any software that takes a kitchen-table e-signature and then requests a deposit is executing these rules, whether it knows it or not.

Financing evidence is almost entirely lender- or vendor-sponsored. The most specific figure is EnerBank's 2018 Brickyard Study, which says offering same-as-cash and low-monthly-payment choices can lift a **25% close rate to 44%** ([EnerBank](https://enerbank.com/blog/boost-close-rate-payment-option-benefits-series)). A single-vendor case study reports close rates on $10K+ jobs rising from 27% to 38% when financing appeared on every quote ([ContractorPlus](https://contractorplus.app/blog/how-a-roofer-closed-41-more-jobs-over-10k-by-offering-financing-on-every)). The practice guidance is consistent and lower-risk. Show a monthly payment on every tier, pre-qualify during the appointment, and keep two or three lenders, because the lowest dealer fees tend to come with the lowest approval rates ([BuildFolio](https://build-folio.com/financing/roofing-contractor-financing-guide/)). The research found no data on dealer fees or approval rates for GreenSky, Hearth, Service Finance or Enhancify.

## Insurance jobs double the workflow and delay cash by months

Storm-restoration roofing runs a second state machine inside the job. The steps are inspection, homeowner files the claim, contingency agreement, adjuster meeting, carrier scope (usually in Xactimate), contractor's own scope (often built from an EagleView ESX file), line-by-line comparison and then supplements. Claims with supplements or re-inspections **"often run four to eight weeks or longer"** ([SubcontractorHub](https://www.subcontractorhub.com/blog/roofing-insurance-claim-process)). Xactimate is the shared language between contractor and carrier. EagleView sells ESX files that import measurements, sketch and line items directly into it, but only with an EagleView One subscription ([EagleView](https://www.eagleview.com/blog/streamline-estimates-in-xactimate-with-eagleview-reports-and-esx-files/)). JobNimbus imports carrier estimates through XactAnalysis, though only on its legacy financials ([JobNimbus help](https://support.jobnimbus.com/how-do-i-enable-the-jobnimbus-integration-with-xactimate)).

A supplement is a request to add **"items omitted, overlooked, under paid or discovered after work begins"** ([The Roof Crafters](https://theroofcrafters.com/insurance-mortgage-check-endorsement/)). The usual roof items are drip edge, starter, ridge, ice and water shield, flashing, decking, code upgrades and overhead and profit ([ARH Foundation](https://www.arhfoundation.org/roofing-supplements-guide)). Overhead and profit is conventionally "10 and 10", and the "three-trade rule" for when it is owed is industry custom, not law ([ClaimSpark](https://claimspark.com/blog/overhead-and-profit-roofing)). Turnaround figures are vendor-sourced and conflict. They range from 1-6 weeks, with one claim of 2-3 weeks when the supplement is submitted in Xactimate format against 6-8 weeks as a PDF ([ProLine](https://useproline.com/average-processing-time-for-roofing-insurance-supplement-approvals/); [OneScope](https://www.onescopellc.com/blog/the-roofing-insurance-supplement-process-explained-for-professional-contractors)). Outsourced supplement firms typically charge **about 10-20% of recovered funds** or $150-$300 flat per claim ([RoofPredict](https://roofpredict-v2.pages.dev/blog/unlock-savings-roofing-supplement-company-vs-in-house)). No independent success-rate data exists. The only figure found is a vendor's "89%."

The money path is the real pain. On a replacement-cost (RCV) policy, the carrier first pays actual cash value: RCV minus depreciation minus the deductible. It **holds back recoverable depreciation until a certificate of completion and a final invoice matching the scope are submitted** ([The Roofing Brief](https://theroofingbrief.com/recoverable-depreciation-roof/)). Roof ACV endorsements or payment schedules can make that depreciation non-recoverable ([Bill Layne Insurance](https://www.billlayneinsurance.com/blog/blogs/acv-roof-claims)). Checks are usually co-payable to the mortgage lender. The lender's loss-draft unit endorses them and often releases funds in thirds, tied to inspections ([Gateway First](https://www.gatewayfirst.com/support/account-help/loss-draft-and-insurance-claims-faq.md)). Fannie Mae's servicing guide lets servicers release an initial disbursement of the greater of $40,000 or 33% of proceeds for current borrowers ([Fannie Mae B-5-01](https://servicing-guide.fanniemae.com/svc/b-5-01/insured-loss-events), read via snippet). Every supplement check re-enters that endorsement loop ([The Roof Crafters](https://theroofcrafters.com/insurance-mortgage-check-endorsement/)). The cited causes of delay are **paperwork friction rather than denials**: wrong endorsements and missing W-9s, lien waivers, licenses or contracts ([UpHelp](https://uphelp.org/ask-an-expert/question/mortgage-company-holding-insurance-check/)). Reps feel it as commission lag, with one vendor anecdote putting the final insurance payment at about 2-3 months out ([Contractors Cloud](https://contractorscloud.com/blog/roofing-sales-commissions-models-examples-payouts-and-how-to-automate-them)).

Compliance risk is high in the hail and hurricane states. Texas, Colorado and Florida prohibit contractors from waiving or rebating deductibles, and in Florida doing so with intent to defraud is a third-degree felony ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/roofing-and-insurance-know-the-law.html); [Colorado Roofing Association](https://coloradoroofing.org/news/waiving-insurance-deductibles-is-illegal-in-colorado); [Fla. Stat. 489.147](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.147)). Kansas and Minnesota restrict it as well ([Kansas HB 2307](https://kslegislature.gov/li_2012/b2011_12/measures/documents/hb2307_00_0000.pdf); [Minnesota session law](https://revisor.mn.gov/laws/2010/0/Session+Law/Chapter/324)). Texas bars a roofer from acting as public adjuster on its own jobs ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/roofing-and-insurance-know-the-law.html)). Florida voided post-loss assignment of benefits for policies issued on or after January 1, 2023 ([Jimerson Birr](https://www.jimersonfirm.com/blog/2023/10/policyholders-are-no-longer-permitted-to-assign-their-insurance-benefits-in-florida/amp/)). Homeowners, for their part, describe door-knockers calling roofs "totaled," contracts that are void unless insurance pays for a full replacement, and depreciation plus deductible roughly halving the first check. That comes from anecdotal Reddit mirrors ([snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tk31y5/snapshots/2026-05-23T02%3A07%3A01.891785Z)). None of the vendors researched shows the homeowner a plain-language claim explainer. That is the clearest unclaimed UX territory in the market.

## The back half leaks margin through orders, change orders and late costs

**Material ordering now runs through three corporate platforms.** QXO closed its roughly $11B purchase of Beacon on April 29, 2025 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250429541973/en/QXO-Completes-Acquisition-of-Beacon-Roofing-Supply)), and PRO+ features are moving to QXO.com ([QXO](https://go.qxo.com/qxo-announcement)). Home Depot's SRS closed its acquisition of GMS on September 4, 2025 ([Home Depot 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)). ABC Supply remains independent. Each runs a contractor portal that turns an estimate or measurement into an order with live branch pricing and delivery photos. AccuLynx converts a signed estimate into an ABC order "in a matter of seconds" ([AccuLynx](https://acculynx.com/integrations/abc-supply/)). SRS Roof Hub plugs into JobNimbus, Leap, Hover and, since 2025, Roofr ([Distribution Strategy](https://distributionstrategy.com/2025/07/srs-launches-roofr-integration-as-home-depot-pushes-further-into-pro-contracting/)). Distributors are also moving up the workflow: SRS launched a homeowner-facing Roof Hub Estimator in October 2024 ([Roofing Contractor](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/100033-srs-distribution-launches-roof-hub-estimator)). Integration does not remove the manual risk points. Someone still has to verify quantities, confirm colors with the homeowner, choose a rooftop load or a ground drop (power lines and narrow driveways prevent roof loads, per a [supplier policy](https://chameleon-avocado-9ywt.squarespace.com/delivery-policy)) and return leftovers before credit windows close. Published restocking fees run from 0% to 40% ([Simonson Lumber](https://simonson-lumber.com/materials/return-policy/)).

**Production scheduling is a rolling two-week calendar gated on readiness.** A production manager posting defines the job as maintaining "a live 2-week rolling install schedule," assigning crews by capacity, adjusting for weather and logging change orders ([posting](https://swooped.co/job-postings/production-manager-remote-assist-world-f80a5)). One vendor says 66% of contractors name weather as their top scheduling risk and that homeowners often are not told about the cascade. The figure is unverified ([Arrivy](https://www.arrivy.com/blog/how-roofing-project-management-software-automates-scheduling-rescheduling/)). Crews are often subcontractors paid per square. Leap models 2026 sub rates from about **$122 per square in Arkansas to about $424 in California** ([Leap price sheet](https://leaptodigital.com/e-books/2026-roofing-subcontractor-price-sheet/)). That means crew pay can be calculated from the same measurement as the estimate. Fall protection was **OSHA's most-cited standard for the 15th straight year in FY2025, with 5,914 violations** ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/top10)), which makes per-job safety photos a defensible checklist item.

**The most common change order is predictable.** Rotten decking found at tear-off is usually pre-priced per sheet in the contract. The Florida Roofing and Sheet Metal Contractors Association (FRSA) template has the customer agree to pay "$___ per sheet of plywood replaced without the need for a written change order" ([FRSA](https://floridaroof.com/Addressing-Self-Adhered-Underlayment-in-Contracts-1-21818.html)), and example rates run $60-$75 per sheet ([Zephyrhills](https://archives.ci.zephyrhills.fl.us/WebLink/0/doc/74761/Page8.aspx)). The field flow is therefore mostly counting: photo, sheet count, unit price, approval, and onto the invoice. CompanyCam's chronological photo timelines and per-job-type checklists with required photos have become the de facto job record, shareable with homeowners as a live link ([CompanyCam](https://companycam.com/resources/blog/how-high-volume-roofers-use-companycam)).

**Collections follow milestones, and accounting sync is the weak joint.** A typical retail schedule is a 10-30% deposit, a progress payment at material delivery or mid-job, and a final payment after inspection and walkthrough, with conditional lien waivers on progress payments and an unconditional waiver on the final ([RoofVista](https://roofvista.com/resources/guides/roof-replacement-payment-schedule)). Lien rights hinge on strict state deadlines. In Texas, for example, missing notice "may be fatal" to the claim ([Levelset TX](https://hub.levelset.com/hubfs/_Levelset/Levelset%20Lien%20and%20Notice%20Fact%20Sheets/TX_FactSheet.pdf)). The JobNimbus help docs show the structural problem. Invoices and payments sync to QuickBooks Online, but **work orders, material orders, vendors and bills paid do not** ([JobNimbus help](https://support.jobnimbus.com/connecting-to-quickbooks-online)). Payments land in Undeposited Funds and duplicate if matched from the bank feed ([JobNimbus help](https://support.jobnimbus.com/how-does-a-payment-sync-from-jobnimbus-to-quickbooks)). Revenue lives in the CRM and costs live in accounting, so job profit lives in a spreadsheet.

**Warranties carry a deadline, and commissions depend on job cost.** GAF may decline to issue Golden Pledge if it is **not registered within 45 days of installation** ([GAF brochure](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/document-library/documents/brochures-&-literature/golden-pledge-limited-warranty-brochure-reswt161b.pdf)). Secondary sites say 30 days, so treat the official 45 as authoritative and other manufacturers' windows as unverified. Rep pay is usually about 8-12% of contract, 20-30% of gross profit, or a storm-market "10/50/50" split ([Contractors Cloud](https://contractorscloud.com/blog/roofing-sales-commissions-models-examples-payouts-and-how-to-automate-them); [Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/roofing-sales-compensation-plans/), snippet). Every late distributor invoice, credit or crew adder therefore delays or disputes a payout. Reps complain of commission checks "without a detailed breakdown" ([Contractors Cloud](https://contractorscloud.com/blog/roofing-sales-commissions-models-examples-payouts-and-how-to-automate-them), vendor anecdote). Owners benchmark gross margin at roughly 25-40% and net margin at 5-15% ([JobNimbus](https://jobnimbus.com/blog/roofing-business-profit-margin)). Review requests work best at completion, through a direct Google link or a QR code with project photos ([CompanyCam](https://companycam.com/resources/blog/how-high-volume-roofers-use-companycam)). No conversion data was found.

## Incumbents hide prices while consolidation and AI reshape the field

The market has six layers. The first is roofing-only all-in-ones (AccuLynx, JobNimbus). The second is sales and proposal-first tools (Leap SalesPro, SumoQuote, Roofle, SalesRabbit). The third is Roofr, which started in measurement and has moved up into CRM. The fourth is point tools (CompanyCam, Hover, EagleView, GAF QuickMeasure, RoofSnap). The fifth is generalist field-service platforms (Jobber for small crews, ServiceTitan for large operators). The sixth is the systems of record, Xactimate and QuickBooks. A full stack for a 5-10 crew residential roofer is estimated at **$800-$1,500 a month** ([Skill Mammoth](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/best-roofing-software)). ## Users forgive missing features but not lost photos or surprise bills

Review sentiment is broadly positive for the roofing-native CRMs. AccuLynx has about 800-850 Capterra reviews, with support **93% positive among the 148 reviews that mention it**, but **pricing about 71% negative across the 66 reviews that mention it** ([Capterra AccuLynx](https://www.capterra.com/p/116187/Acculynx/reviews/)). JobNimbus scores 4.6 on both Capterra and G2 ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/jobnimbus-vs-quick-bid-vs-acculynx)). The angriest reviews are about commercial terms, not features. A Leap user writes that "the price hike became 3x what I used to pay and they forced me to pay it for two months," and another paid four more months after cancelling in writing ([Capterra Leap](https://www.capterra.com/p/162690/Leap/reviews/)). A ServiceTitan reviewer is "no longer using the software while remaining obligated under the contract" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/servicetitan/reviews)). A Roofr reviewer reports a subscription he could not cancel ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/construction/roofr-profile/reviews/)). These are review excerpts seen only as search snippets. Their wording and dates should be checked before anyone quotes them.

The most consistent UX failure is mobile photo capture. AccuLynx reviewers say the app "often crashes for all our employees" ([Capterra p.4](https://www.capterra.com/p/116187/Acculynx/reviews/?page=4)) and that "downloading and taking pictures takes forever on the app" ([Capterra p.9](https://www.capterra.com/p/116187/Acculynx/reviews/?page=9)). A 4-star App Store reviewer says it "sometimes freezes or crashes, especially when uploading multiple photos" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/acculynx-field/id1485611392)). CompanyCam uploads reportedly stall in rural areas ([roofingsoftwareguide](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/companycam-review/), affiliate), and Roofr's web app reportedly has no offline mode ([fervorstudio](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/roofr-review-pricing-alternatives/), secondary). The positive mirror is equally telling. A roofing estimator praises CompanyCam photos that sort automatically into the AccuLynx customer profile ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/field-service/companycam-profile/)). Zero-effort handoff is what users notice and love. Measurement trust is the next issue: a Roofr reviewer says pitches are "regularly incorrect" ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/construction/roofr-profile/reviews/)), and tree cover defeats aerial imagery. Learning curve and complexity recur for JobNimbus and AccuLynx ([Capterra JobNimbus](https://capterra.com/p/126797/JobNimbus/reviews/?page=4)). On the RoofersCoffeeShop forum, posters say "we have tried a few but have not really been happy," "sometimes simple is best," and "most of them do very little support and training" ([RCS forum](https://www.rooferscoffeeshop.com/forum/topic/what-is-roofing-company-using-for-their-crm)). Another asks whether a one-stop solution exists or whether you must stitch tools together ([RCS forum](https://www.rooferscoffeeshop.com/forum/topic/best-crm-software)).

By role, the evidence is uneven. **Owners** care about add-on creep, per-seat growth, contract risk, QuickBooks sync and job-costing and P&L visibility. JobNimbus's own comparison page concedes that AccuLynx has job costing, P&L and commissions that JobNimbus lacks ([JobNimbus](https://www.jobnimbus.com/comparison/jobnimbus-vs-acculynx)). **Office admins** complain of re-entering third-party bookings by hand, getting no notification when an email arrives on a job, "cumbersome" invoicing and a buggy calendar. These are single-review anecdotes ([Capterra AccuLynx](https://www.capterra.com/p/116187/Acculynx/reviews/)). **Sales reps** want a fast estimate at the table, same-visit capture of the kind Hover offers, and transparent, timely commissions. **Crew leads** need photo upload that works and instructions in their language, and both Roofr and AccuLynx now ship Spanish work orders ([Roofr](https://roofr.com/product-blog/new-in-roofr-spanish-work-orders); [AccuLynx](https://acculynx.com/mobile-crew-app/)). That is vendor evidence of demand. No first-hand crew quotes were found.

**Homeowners** rank price clarity first, then trust signals, then communication. A Roofr survey of 292 homeowners found that **74% would not hire a roofer without an inspection first** and that homeowners would pay more to be kept informed ([Roofr](https://roofr.com/masterclass/what-do-homeowners-want-from-roofers), vendor). BBB complaints cluster on deposits taken for work never done, poor workmanship, debris and deductible billing disputes ([News Tribune / BBB](https://www.newstribune.com/news/business/story/2020/apr/05/bbb-tips-think-safety-first-when-hiring-roofers/823221/), 2020 data). Channel preference is hybrid. **58% prefer to book home services by phone** ([Tinuiti](https://reports.tinuiti.com/tinuiti-home-services-marketing-study/page-4.html)), while texts win for reminders and updates ([EZ Texting](https://www.eztexting.com/report/2024-consumer-texting-report), vendor). The best customer-tracking pattern comes from solar, not roofing. Freedom Forever's portal shows completed milestones in solid green and future ones outlined ([Freedom Forever](https://intercom.help/freedomforever/en/articles/12045186-how-to-read-the-customer-portal-timeline-self-help-guide-for-project-updates)). Reonic lets the installer choose which internal statuses the customer sees and advances them automatically ([Reonic](https://docs.reonic.com/docs/en/settings-customer-communication-progress-tracking)). Housecall Pro's one-tap "On my way" texts the customer, and the customer's replies route back to the tech ([Housecall Pro](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/357613-customer-notifications-overview)). Field-UX guidance converges on offline-first sync with explicit states ("Offline – will sync later"), tested by switching on airplane mode and then capturing a photo, note and signature ([GeekyAnts](https://customer-experience.geekyants.com/book/it_services/chapter_21.md)). It also stresses high contrast for sunlight and redesigned, not merely enlarged, controls for gloves ([Apario](https://blog.apario.net/on-writing-software-for-people-who-work-outside)).

## Prioritized recommendations for Ridge & Beam

### Where the current product stands

Ridge & Beam's schema (`supabase/migrations/20261106000000_init.sql`) and calculator (`src/lib/roof.ts`) already get several things right. The calculator produces separate takeoffs for starter, drip edge, ridge cap, underlayment, ice and water, nails and pipe boots, and it rounds up to whole bundles. That prevents the "forgot the accessories" class of error. Lead source is captured on customers, and public tokenized links exist for both estimates (`/q/[token]`) and invoices (`/i/[token]`). The structural gaps, set against the evidence above, are these. Insurance fields (carrier, claim number, adjuster) sit on the **customer** record rather than the job, so a repeat customer with a second claim, or a rental owner with two properties, has nowhere to put it. The `job_stage` enum has no insurance stages. A single waste percentage applies across all roof sections, low-slope included. Estimates have one option, and acceptance records only a typed name. Payments are a flat list with an `insurance` method and no notion of expected versus received checks, deductible or depreciation. Roles are limited to owner, admin and member. The recommendations below are ordered by value against effort. Each is mapped to the module it changes and graded by evidence: **Strong** means statute, lender guide, manufacturer document or vendor help documentation. **Moderate** means several independent or practitioner sources agree. **Thin or vendor** means single-vendor claims, affiliate sites or snippet-only review excerpts.

### How much to trust this evidence

The recommendations rest unevenly on the evidence. The firmest are the ones grounded in law and documents: state deposit and cancellation rules, deductible-waiver bans, the RCV/ACV and loss-draft mechanics, GAF's 45-day registration window, the EagleView report schema, JobNimbus's documented QuickBooks gaps and the OSHA citation counts. Those justify N2, N4, N5, X10 and X11 with little further research. The user-sentiment patterns (lost photos, contract anger, price opacity) recur across many review excerpts, but none could be read in full, verified by date or attributed to a reviewer role. They are strong enough to direct design, not to quote in marketing. Almost every conversion and uplift number is vendor- or lender-sourced. That covers financing nearly doubling close rates, the middle-tier preference, warranty lift, speed-to-lead multipliers, instant-quote conversion, supplement approval rates and the weather-risk percentage. Some of them conflict. Software prices come mostly from affiliate or competitor review sites and disagree with each other, sometimes by double. The weakest areas are first-hand owner and office-admin pain, crew language and tooling, weather rescheduling practice, steep-roof phone ergonomics, review conversion and why small shops stay on whiteboards. Reddit, Facebook groups and full review pages were unreachable. Five to ten ride-alongs and interviews, with one owner, one office admin, one rep and one crew lead per company, would validate X1, X5, X6 and X9 more cheaply than building them on assumption.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "amazing UX" means for roofers. The best experience is not a prettier pipeline board. It is the absence of re-entry and of surprise. Competitors have converged on similar feature lists, and AI receptionists became parity within a year. The durable differentiators are structural. One measurement schema should flow into tiers, orders, crew pay, invoices and commissions. The claim should be a first-class object with its own money ledger. Contracts should obey state law by default. A field app should treat a lost photo as a severity-one bug. Each of these removes a handoff where today's users either type numbers again or lose trust. Ridge & Beam's young codebase is an advantage here. Moving claims to jobs, adding tiers, and replacing the flat payments table with an expected-versus-received ledger is cheap now and very expensive after a thousand tenants have data in the old shape.

The second insight is that the homeowner is an unclaimed user. Every vendor builds for the contractor and gives the homeowner a PDF and a signature box. Homeowners say they would pay more to be kept informed, their worst experiences involve insurance confusion and vanished contractors, and solar has already shown the milestone-tracker pattern. A roofer who can text a link showing the license, the crew, the tear-off photos and "your second check arrives after we send the completion certificate" sells against storm chasers without saying a word about them. Combined with flat, cancel-anytime pricing in a market whose loudest complaints are about lock-in, that points to a positioning that is both honest and hard for incumbents to copy: the roofing platform that both the roofer and the homeowner can trust.
